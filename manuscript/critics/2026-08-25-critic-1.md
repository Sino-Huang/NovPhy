### HIGH — The central comparison is not operationally stable across the drafting documents

- **Locations:** `manuscript/research_evidence.md:9`; `manuscript/content_brief.md:27`; `manuscript/reviewer_expectations_2026.md:19`; `manuscript/teaser_figure_brief.md:29-35,126`.
- **Offending text:** The ledger asks whether the controller improves over “**fixed and factorized policies**,” whereas the content brief requires improvement over “**the four controller-free baselines and the parameter-matched two-head controller**.” The teaser then calls all six entries a “**Prospective matched-compute comparison**” and says they feed a “**common final interface**.”
- **Why this is unsafe:** “Fixed and factorized” does not identify the four controller-free policies, while the figure’s phrase “matched-compute comparison” visually applies to all six. Yet the evidence ledger explicitly says issue-9 baselines are *not* compute-matched to issue 10. The briefs correctly prohibit using existing issue-9/10 evidence as such a comparison, but they do not state whether the *planned* final protocol will recompute/match every listed baseline or whether the four policies are merely required comparators. A drafter can therefore turn an unresolved protocol into a claim that a matched suite already exists.
- **Required correction:** Use one enumerated comparator set and one explicit prospective condition everywhere: the four named controller-free policies plus the parameter-matched two-head controller, all evaluated only after a predeclared common-compute protocol is authorized. Do not retain the underspecified “fixed and factorized policies” shorthand.

### HIGH — The teaser replaces the only concrete horizon specification with undefined qualitative bins

- **Locations:** `manuscript/CONTEXT.md:61-69`; `manuscript/research_evidence.md:17`; `manuscript/teaser_figure_brief.md:23,70,95`.
- **Offending text:** The evidence defines the surface as horizons “**`(1, 5, 15)`**,” but the prescribed figure grid has rows “**`short`, `medium`, `long`**”; its legend says only that `\Delta` is a “**Requested prediction horizon in fixed steps**.”
- **Why this is unsafe:** This is not a harmless visual simplification. It removes the actual 3-by-3 evaluated contract and leaves the mapping from qualitative labels to fixed steps unstated. A future draft can describe an adaptive continuum or different bins while still appearing to depict the verified surface.
- **Required correction:** Put `1`, `5`, and `15` fixed steps in the grid (or state directly on the figure that short/medium/long mean 1/5/15 fixed steps). Keep “requested,” not observed/effective, horizon terminology.

### MEDIUM — The figure’s endpoint strip conflates a specified primary endpoint with two ancillary endpoint checks

- **Locations:** `manuscript/content_brief.md:29-30`; `manuscript/research_evidence.md:19,29,33`; `manuscript/teaser_figure_brief.md:35,81,126`.
- **Offending text:** The figure places “**terminal outcome [TODO: result]**” next to “**two endpoint checks**,” while its caption says the planned study uses a “**common matched-compute interface**.” Elsewhere, terminal-outcome accuracy is the **Specified primary endpoint**, while the two available measurements are only violation checks for excess penetration and unsupported stationary/floating bodies.
- **Why this matters:** The compact strip makes the two physical checks look like coequal pieces of the common endpoint despite their explicitly endpoint-only, non-dense-path scope. This invites a future caption or result panel to substitute a violation rate for terminal-outcome accuracy, precisely the claim-evidence swap the ledger is meant to prevent.
- **Required correction:** Label terminal-outcome accuracy as the *specified primary endpoint* and label the two checks as *endpoint-only diagnostics*, or omit the diagnostic token until the final interface is defined.

### MEDIUM — The figure’s `two-head` label drops the parameter-matching constraint that makes the factorized comparison interpretable

- **Locations:** `manuscript/CONTEXT.md:73-77`; `manuscript/research_evidence.md:22`; `manuscript/teaser_figure_brief.md:31,33,79,126`.
- **Offending text:** The canonical documents specify a “**parameter-matched two-head controller**,” but the exact on-figure comparator is merely “**two-head**.”
- **Why this matters:** Parameter matching is the only stated control against attributing a capacity difference to joint selection. The caption restores the qualifier, but a skimming reviewer will read the strip, not the caption. The shortened label weakens the causal interpretation before any final result exists.
- **Required correction:** Use “parameter-matched two-head” in the figure, or attach a legible “parameter-matched” qualifier directly to that label.

### LOW — The evidence ledger buries its most easily misquoted null result in an overloaded sentence

- **Location:** `manuscript/research_evidence.md:22`.
- **Offending text:** “**The controllers have equal scores, with no observed advantage for the joint controller. The recorded endpoint violation rate is `0.0002777777777777778`.**”
- **Why this matters:** The rate has no named denominator, split, policy attribution, or indication of whether it is pooled. In the same row as the equal-score result, it is easy to misreport as a comparative physical-plausibility result. The surrounding boundary says it is model-selection only, but the numerical detail remains less scannable than the boundary.
- **Required correction:** Attach the rate to its measured population and aggregation (or omit it from planning prose); visually separate it from the controller-score statement.

## Verdict

**WEAK REJECT** as a safe basis for drafting a central-efficacy paper. The documents are unusually disciplined about evidence status, but the central comparison still shifts from an underspecified “fixed and factorized” claim to a visually all-inclusive matched-compute suite. That ambiguity is the single most damaging issue because it can manufacture the very comparison the evidence ledger says does not yet exist.
