# Critic 1 Review — 2026-09-19

**Scope.** This review covers only the title, Abstract, and written Section 1 Introduction in `manuscript/iclr2026/iclr2026_conference.tex` (lines 14 and 57–97). I checked the reported confirmatory claims against the issue-15 confirmatory summary, collection summary, and prospective statistical protocol named in the review request. Sections 2–6 are not started and are not reviewed as prose.

## Evidence check

The numerical and decision claims are, in the narrow sense, accurately transcribed from the evidence.

- The protocol defines the primary estimand as the within-complete-rollout mean **duration-weighted authoritative endpoint carrier MSE over eligible teacher-forced decision states**, with candidate and strongest comparator paired within rollout. This agrees with P8 (l.86), except that the Abstract compresses away “authoritative” and “teacher-forced eligible states.”
- Gain is **strongest-comparator error minus candidate error**. Thus the higher-budget mean of `-0.000520526...` and 95% interval `[-0.000709082..., -0.000362510...]` support the rounded Abstract/P9 values and mean that the candidate is worse, not better.
- The strongest comparator is `matched_capacity_two_head` at the lower budget and `fixed_pair` at the higher budget. The result summary reports six paired complete rollouts at both budgets; the collection record independently reports six accepted sealed rollouts, all terminating `stable_entered`.
- The zero lower-budget gain and zero violation increase are correctly reported. The physical measure is, however, specifically within-rollout **authoritative source-endpoint incidence of any accepted endpoint violation**; recursive predicted-carrier violation is unavailable.
- The protocol’s `not_supported_by_this_experiment` outcome is correctly bounded to instance-held-out configurations in the same physics family and accepted ontology. It expressly excludes template-held-out generalization, shifted physics, material/damage semantics, illegal-contact derivation, and planning/gameplay success.

## Issues

### CRITICAL — The reported endpoint is teacher-forced carrier error, but the Abstract presents the result as a general test of action-consequence prediction

**Location:** Abstract, l.58; Introduction P8–P9, ll.86–89.

**Offending text:** The Abstract says “**World models predict the consequences of actions**” and reports “**duration-weighted endpoint carrier MSE**,” while P8 specifies “**authoritative endpoint carrier MSE over eligible teacher-forced decision states**.”

The evidence supports the latter, much narrower estimand—not free-running action-consequence prediction, planning performance, gameplay success, or even recursive predicted-carrier physical validity. Omitting both *authoritative* and *teacher-forced eligible states* from the Abstract makes the headline negative result sound like a broader world-model failure test than it is. This is an Abstract blocker because endpoint semantics are the entire content of the reported result, not a methodological footnote.

### HIGH — The safety statement is technically true but leaves the decisive scope invisible

**Location:** Abstract, l.58; Introduction P8–P9, ll.86–89.

**Offending text:** “**with no increase in authoritative endpoint physical-violation incidence**” and “**Authoritative endpoint physical-violation incidence did not increase at either budget.**”

The evidence does support zero candidate-minus-comparator increase, but only for the within-rollout incidence of accepted violations at the authoritative **source endpoint**. The protocol explicitly says “recursive predicted-carrier violation is unavailable.” The prose never gives a reader this boundary; “physical-violation incidence” invites the far stronger inference that generated trajectories were physically no worse. This must not be allowed to function as a generic physical-plausibility claim.

### HIGH — The Abstract is unusually candid but not yet submission-ready because its result sentence overloads every essential qualifier

**Location:** Abstract, l.58.

**Offending text:** “**At the higher budget, the mean paired gain against the fixed-pair comparator was $-5.2 \times 10^{-4}$ (95\% interval $[-7.1 \times 10^{-4}, -3.6 \times 10^{-4}]$), where gain is comparator error minus candidate error, with no increase in authoritative endpoint physical-violation incidence.**”

This single sentence forces the reader to parse budget selection, comparator identity, a sign convention opposite to the usual candidate-minus-baseline effect, an interval, and a safety qualifier. The negative sign is correct, but the prose does not immediately state its interpretation: joint selection had higher error than fixed-pair. A reviewer skimming the Abstract can easily retain a negative numerical value without retaining whether it is an improvement or degradation. Concision is not achieved by compressing the result’s interpretation out of the sentence.

### MEDIUM — “Strongest eligible comparator” is accurate but potentially misleading without the selection rule

**Location:** Abstract, l.58; Introduction P8, l.86.

**Offending text:** “**the strongest eligible comparator at each of two compute-support budgets**” and “**the frozen decision compares the candidate only with the strongest eligible comparator.**”

This matches the protocol and the evidence: two-head is selected at the lower budget and fixed-pair at the higher. But a reader cannot tell from the Abstract why different baselines are used at different budgets, or that the full five-comparator matrix was recorded but the frozen primary comparison selects one comparator. Without that context, the result can look post hoc comparator shopping even though the protocol says otherwise. This is a communication flaw, not an evidence contradiction.

### MEDIUM — The Abstract’s negative result is a coherent bounded finding, but it is not compelling enough to carry the title’s broad method promise

**Location:** Title, l.14; Abstract, l.58; Introduction P9, l.89.

**Offending text:** “**Granularity as a Decision: World Models with Joint Horizon--Description Selection**,” followed by “**Joint granularity selection was not supported by this experiment**” and “**adaptivity gave no benefit here**.”

The title is not factually false: a joint-selection controller was implemented and tested. But it advertises a methodological advance while the only completed confirmatory result says the candidate ties the strongest comparator at one budget and is worse than fixed-pair at the other. The bounded negative result is publishable only if the paper makes the evaluation itself—not an implied successful selection mechanism—the intellectual contribution. As written, the Abstract has no surprising diagnostic, failure analysis, or transferable lesson beyond “this did not help here,” so excitement remains weak.

### MEDIUM — The description-mode axis still lacks a causal rationale, making the negative result hard to interpret

**Location:** Introduction P5–P6, ll.77–80; P8, l.86.

**Offending text:** “**each phase of such a cascade asks for different prediction granularity**”; “**output-description mode ... selects which supervised prediction target is queried and scored**”; and “**A continuous latent always remains the sole rollout carrier.**”

The Introduction never explains what changes in inference, training, available information, or resource use when the controller selects `micro` rather than `macro`, given that the continuous latent is always the rollout state. The result therefore cannot distinguish a failed adaptive-granularity hypothesis from a mode selector that had no consequential lever. This is an existing Introduction problem, distinct from the accurate Abstract result reporting.

### MEDIUM — Paragraph 3’s taxonomy does not establish the claimed novelty boundary

**Location:** Introduction P3, l.71.

**Offending text:** “**Other lines vary the description side alone**” includes “**fixed hierarchical prediction across multiple time scales**” and “**fixed short-detailed versus long-simplified segments**,” then concludes “**None of these lines jointly treats horizon and output-description mode**.”

The cited categories themselves sound temporally coupled. The text may intend to contrast a fixed cross-scale schedule with a state-dependent joint request, but it never makes that distinction before asserting a universal negative. This unresolved taxonomy issue is not an Abstract blocker, but it weakens the paper’s claim that its failed test isolates a genuinely untested intervention.

### LOW — The common literature premise remains too broad for the support supplied in the written Introduction

**Location:** Abstract, l.58; Introduction P2, l.68.

**Offending text:** “**most systems fix the horizon and output description of a prediction at design time**,” while P2 says “**This task-specific design is a widespread default, not a universal rule.**”

The hedged P2 wording is defensible; “most systems” in the Abstract is stronger and not independently substantiated there. This is less serious than the endpoint-semantic omission, but the Abstract should not sharpen the literature claim beyond the careful qualification in the Introduction.

## What is working

- **Abstract, l.58:** The exact sign convention is stated: “**gain is comparator error minus candidate error**.” It agrees with the protocol and makes the reported negative interval internally consistent.
- **P8–P9, ll.86–89:** The strongest comparator, six within-rollout pairs, two budgets, and bounded `not supported` conclusion agree with the checked records.
- **P9, l.89:** “**It does not address template-held-out generalization, shifted physics, or gameplay success**” faithfully respects the protocol’s exclusion boundary. “**does not assert that joint selection cannot help**” is likewise warranted.
- **Abstract, l.58:** The result is candid, avoids manufacturing a positive conclusion from a tie or a negative gain, and is substantially more informative than the previous placeholder abstract.

## Missing manuscript content

Sections 2–6 are not started; this review does not assess them as prose, but their absence makes the current document incomplete as a conference submission.

## Verdict: REJECT

**Single most damaging issue:** The headline evidence is duration-weighted authoritative endpoint **teacher-forced** carrier MSE, yet the Abstract frames it as a general test of world models predicting action consequences. That semantic gap invites a claim substantially broader than the verified endpoint supports.
