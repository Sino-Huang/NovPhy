# Final Academic Critic Review — 2026-08-31

**Scope.** Reviewed only the title (TeX line 12), temporary abstract (lines 55–57), and Section 1 Introduction (lines 59–83) in `manuscript/iclr2026/iclr2026_conference.tex`, against the specified manuscript records. Sections 2–6 are not reviewed because they do not exist.

## CRITICAL

- **The paper has no completed, authorized test of its title-level claim, and the Introduction makes this fatal rather than merely incomplete.** The manuscript says the central question is whether the controller “**improves terminal-outcome prediction relative to five named comparators**” (Introduction, line 72), but immediately concedes that “**That readout and its shared coordinate decoder are specified but not completed**” (line 72) and that “**The central comparative result remains [TODO: result]**” (line 74). This is not a normal limitation: the primary endpoint is not completed and the only experiment that could answer the claimed contribution has neither an authorized metric nor a result. The abstract repeats the same prospective framing: “**The planned evaluation compares**” (abstract, line 56). An ICLR/NeurIPS/ICML empirical-method paper cannot be a proposal whose claimed mechanism is untested. The bounded artifacts explicitly do not rescue it. **Correction:** do not submit this as an empirical controller paper until the authorized common-compute comparison and completed common endpoint exist; otherwise retitle and rescope the manuscript as a formulation/infrastructure/protocol paper, with no implication of a validated benefit.

- **The Introduction opens by asserting the very normative conclusion the paper admits it cannot establish.** “**Prediction granularity should be chosen at each decision point, not fixed when a world model is trained**” (Introduction, line 62) is a categorical conclusion, not a question or design hypothesis. It is irreconcilable with “**Nothing in this paper should be read as a demonstrated advantage for that controller**” (line 70) and “**They do not include a demonstrated empirical advantage**” (line 78). The issue-10 result also reports equal controller scores, not supporting evidence for “should.” This is exactly the sort of conclusion-first framing that reviewers will identify as hype masking an absent result. **Correction:** replace the declarative opening with a conditional research question (e.g., whether state-dependent selection can better match differing predictive demands) and reserve any normative conclusion for authorized comparative evidence.

## HIGH

- **“The final test … is sealed” materially misstates what is sealed and obscures the unexecuted comparison.** The manuscript states, “**The final test of this question is sealed and awaits authorization. Six final-evaluation rollouts have been collected and sealed**” (Introduction, line 74). Per the evidence boundary, the *rollouts* are sealed; the predeclared common-compute comparison still requires recomputing every comparator after authorization. Calling the unperformed test “sealed” lets readers infer that a complete test exists but is merely embargoed. The abstract’s “**completion of the sealed evaluation**” (line 56) compounds the ambiguity: the collection is complete, while authorization, metric derivation/consumption, the shared evaluator, and the central comparison are not. **Correction:** say precisely that six *final-evaluation rollouts* are collected and sealed, and that authorization is pending before final metric derivation and the predeclared comparator evaluation; remove “final test … is sealed” and “completion of the sealed evaluation.”

- **The claimed intervention is conceptually unstable: “description mode” is sold as representation selection, but defined as a readout request over an invariant representation.** The abstract motivates the work by saying models use a fixed “**representation**” and proposes a joint choice of “**prediction horizon and description mode**” (abstract, line 56). Yet the Introduction defines mode as “**the requested description of the prediction itself**” (line 64), then says “**a continuous predictive latent remains the sole carrier of the rollout**” and “**Micro and macro outputs are readouts over that latent, never recurrent state**” (line 68). Thus the method apparently does *not* select the rollout representation; it selects a horizon and an output/readout target while retaining one latent representation. That can be a valid contribution, but it is not the stronger “horizon--representation selection” premise. A reviewer cannot tell whether the controller changes prediction objective, decoder, supervision, computational path, or representational state. **Correction:** use one term consistently—either explicitly frame this as joint horizon-and-output-description/readout selection, or define and demonstrate a genuine change in predictive representation. State what operationally changes for each of the nine pairs.

- **The literature positioning contains unsupported universal claims and literal missing citations at the exact point novelty is asserted.** “**Many latent world models commit to a single prediction horizon and a single output representation at design time [TODO: cite]**” and “**Whether a world model should decide how far to predict, and at what descriptive granularity, as one coupled per-decision choice remains open**” (Introduction, line 66) are field-wide prevalence and novelty claims with no citations. The benchmark anchor is likewise “**published NovPhy benchmark … [TODO: cite]**” (line 70). The abstract’s “**latent world models typically predict using a fixed horizon and representation**” (line 56) makes the same uncited, overbroad premise. This is not a cosmetic bibliography defect: without support, the entire straw-man contrast and claimed gap are unsubstantiated. **Correction:** replace every literal TODO before circulation; cite specific world-model and temporal-abstraction work, delimit the claim to the cited families, and phrase novelty narrowly as a gap the paper proposes to test rather than an unqualified “remains open.”

- **The abstract and Introduction do not present the comparator set with consistent, auditable precision.** The abstract promises “**fixed, single-axis, independent-axis, and parameter-matched factorized alternatives**” (line 56), whereas the Introduction correctly names “**fixed-pair**,” “**temporal-only**,” “**description-only**,” “**independent-axes**,” and “**a parameter-matched two-head controller**” (line 72). “Single-axis” collapses two materially distinct controls; plural “factorized alternatives” can be read as more than the one two-head controller. Since the protocol is the manuscript’s main deliverable, its central falsification set must not change granularity between abstract and Introduction. **Correction:** enumerate all five comparators in the abstract exactly as in line 72, including that the first four are controller-free and the fifth is one parameter-matched two-head factorization.

## MEDIUM

- **The mechanism’s motivating dynamics are asserted as empirical payoffs without evidence, then used to manufacture “mismatch.”** The Introduction says, “**Early steps reward fine temporal resolution and object-level detail. Late steps reward only a coarse judgment**” (line 62), followed by a universal inability claim: “**it cannot be well matched to all of them at once**” (line 62). No cited result, formal tradeoff, or completed ablation supports either statement. A sufficiently expressive fixed-horizon, continuous-latent model might produce all stated readouts; indeed line 68 says precisely one continuous carrier is retained. **Correction:** label these sentences as an intuition/hypothesis rather than a reward fact or impossibility claim, and make the eventual diagnostic falsifiable (compare performance by cascade phase and selected pair against fixed-pair and each one-axis control under matched compute).

- **The long bounded-evidence paragraph is honest but badly placed and makes the Introduction read like an audit log rather than a scientific argument.** The 190-word block beginning “**Three bounded artifacts exist, and none answers the central question**” (Introduction, line 76) spends the reader’s first page on issue numbers, 1,600 states, 75 trials, 44 requests, six rollouts, 109 decisions, oracle-state deltas, and repeated non-results. Crucially, “**Every system recorded zero successes**” and “**adaptive granularity was not materially exercised**” are valid bounded issue-57 facts, but they are evidence of a failed gameplay matrix, not motivation or evidence for joint granularity. Likewise, issue-10 equal scores are a relevant negative control result, but not a substitute for the central test. The exhaustive disclaimer forestalls overclaiming while simultaneously advertises that the proposed method has not worked in the only gameplay test. **Correction:** retain one concise Introduction disclosure: existing model-selection/gameplay artifacts are bounded and do not answer the predeclared question. Move the per-issue counts, zero-success floor, usage collapse, and oracle-continuation details to a dedicated experiments/evidence section once drafted; there, report them with their scope and disposition.

- **Method-infrastructure jargon interrupts the contribution story before the paper has defined it.** “**The issue-60 deployment temporal carrier supplies the aligned agent-observable context … under role-isolated scenario lineages**” (Introduction, line 68) and “**deployment-aligned temporal carrier that enforces agent-observable inputs and role-isolated scenario lineages**” (line 81) introduce “issue-60,” “role-isolated,” and “lineages” without defining them for readers. It also makes data-governance implementation sound central to the algorithmic contribution despite the sentence “**That carrier is implemented method infrastructure, not an empirical result**” (line 68). **Correction:** in the Introduction, state only the reader-relevant input-isolation property in plain language; defer issue number, lineage machinery, and carrier contract to Method. Do not allow infrastructure provenance to crowd out the controller’s actual decision rule.

## LOW

- **The title’s declarative half overstates the current evidence.** “**Granularity Is a Decision**” (title, line 12) is memorable but reads as an established prescription, while the paper’s own contribution list says no empirical advantage exists (Introduction, line 78). The subtitle helps, but not enough to neutralize the conclusion in a title-only review queue. **Correction:** if the empirical result remains blocked, use a hypothesis/proposal framing rather than an asserted conclusion.

- **The contribution list contains a subtle status overstatement.** “**six sealed final-evaluation rollouts awaiting authorization**” (Introduction, line 82) is accurate about collection, but under the heading “**Protocol**” it can still make the reader assume the final study itself is ready. The preceding line 72 says the endpoint is not completed. **Correction:** distinguish sealed collected rollouts from the unfinished common readout and pending authorized comparator execution in the same bullet.

## Verdict

**REJECT.** The manuscript’s single most damaging issue is that it frames a strong empirical controller claim while its primary evaluator and authorized five-comparator result are explicitly incomplete; no amount of transparent caveating converts a planned test into an ICLR-level empirical contribution.
