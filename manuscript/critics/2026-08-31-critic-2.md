## CRITICAL

- **The paper is still nonviable as an empirical ICLR submission because its sole central result and its primary evaluator do not exist in completed, authorized form.** The paper defines its central claim as whether the controller “**improves terminal-outcome prediction relative to five named comparators**” (Introduction, line 72), then states “**That readout and its shared coordinate decoder are specified but not completed**” (line 72), “**The comparators have not been executed under the predeclared protocol**,” and “**The central comparative result remains [TODO: result]**” (line 74). The abstract confirms this is only a plan: “**The planned evaluation compares**” (line 56). This is not a limitation around an otherwise established result. The endpoint, authorized metric derivation, matched comparison, statistical analysis, and result underlying the title’s question are all absent. The bounded equal-score and zero-success artifacts cannot answer the question by the paper’s own admission (line 76). **Correction:** do not submit as an empirical latent-world-model/controller contribution until the common evaluator is complete and the authorized five-comparator study yields an analyzable result. Otherwise explicitly rescope the paper to formulation/infrastructure/protocol work and remove all performance-oriented framing.

## HIGH

- **The claimed literature gap remains unsupported at every place where the paper asks readers to accept its premise.** The abstract says “**many latent world models predict at a single fixed horizon and output description [TODO: cite]**” (line 56). The Introduction repeats “**Many latent world models commit to a single prediction horizon and a single output description at design time [TODO: cite]**,” characterizes temporal abstraction with another “**[TODO: cite]**,” and calls the resulting distinction “**the gap this paper proposes to test**” (line 66). Even the benchmark identification ends with “**[TODO: cite]**” (line 70). These are not harmless placeholders: they are the only stated justification that the problem is novel rather than an obvious combination of multi-step prediction and decoder/task routing. The revised wording is less absolute than the previous draft, but an uncited “many” remains a field-wide prevalence claim. **Correction:** insert verified citations before treating this as reviewable prose; constrain “many” to the cited model families and substantiate why existing adaptive-horizon, multi-task/readout, hierarchical, or conditional-compute mechanisms do not already instantiate the proposed choice.

- **The paper now admits that mode selects a readout, which exposes a much weaker and still under-specified intervention than the title/novelty framing implies.** It defines mode as “**the requested output description**” and says “**Mode selects which readout of the carrier the prediction returns, not the state that carries the rollout**” (Introduction, line 64). Likewise, “**Micro and macro outputs are readouts over that latent, never recurrent state**” (line 68). This resolves the prior representation/readout contradiction, but now the contribution is not adaptive representational granularity; it is choosing one of three outputs from a fixed continuous rollout state. The Introduction never says what varies computationally or statistically when a micro versus macro readout is selected, why returning one readout prevents recovering the others, or why this choice can improve the common terminal endpoint rather than merely change reporting. “Joint Horizon--Description Selection” risks sounding like a substantive multi-resolution world-model transition mechanism when the written account describes decoder routing. **Correction:** define, in reader-facing terms, the operational consequence and training/prediction loss for each requested mode, and make the title/claim say output-readout selection if that is all the controller controls. If the contribution is only routing among readouts, justify its nontriviality against a fixed carrier with all heads evaluated jointly.

- **The claimed “common-compute protocol” is a label rather than a falsifiable fairness guarantee in the only text that exists.** The abstract promises a “**predeclared common-compute protocol**” (line 56); the Introduction repeats “**one predeclared common-compute protocol**” (line 72), but supplies no compute unit, budget, matching rule, or account of how policies choosing horizons 1, 5, and 15 receive comparable opportunities to predict. This omission is acute because horizons alter both the number of controller decisions and potential rollout operations; a joint selector could gain merely by a different allocation of computation. The phrase “**matched retraining and evaluation tooling used to assemble the comparator systems**” (line 68) is infrastructure provenance, not a compute-matching specification. **Correction:** before claiming a common-compute comparison, state the matched budget and accounting principle (controller calls, rollout transitions, decoder calls, training compute, and/or wall-clock as applicable), and explain how each of the five controls obeys it. Without this, the headline comparison is not interpretable even if it is eventually run.

## MEDIUM

- **The revised status paragraph is accurate but still reads as an internal project checklist, not an Introduction, and buries the actual scientific narrative under administrative incompleteness.** “**The prespecified replicate analysis, the reproducible archive, the consolidated report, and the terminal program audit also remain open**” (Introduction, line 74) tells an external reviewer about internal work items without clarifying the mechanism or evidence. It follows the important endpoint/authorization disclosure, but escalates that disclosure into a list of unfinished program governance. This invites the obvious conclusion that the work is not ready while consuming scarce introductory space. **Correction:** retain the two scientifically material facts—the endpoint is unfinished and the authorized comparison has not executed—in the Introduction; defer archive/report/audit and replicate-analysis workflow status to a limitations or experimental-protocol account if and when those sections are written.

- **The evidence disclosure is vastly improved in placement and scope, but the prose still risks misleading readers about the negative gameplay finding.** The compressed paragraph says a “**frozen held-out gameplay matrix recorded zero successes for every evaluated system**” (Introduction, line 76). “Every evaluated system” is technically bounded by the preceding “frozen held-out gameplay matrix,” but the sentence does not state that this was five systems, five levels, and three seeds, nor does it give the recorded disposition. A skimming reviewer may reasonably read it as an across-method impossibility or a robust negative result, despite the following qualification that adaptivity was not exercised. **Correction:** either omit the zero-success result from the Introduction entirely and retain only “existing bounded artifacts do not answer the question,” or add enough scope in the same sentence to prevent an apparent general negative claim (five systems on five held-out levels, three seeds; no controller-effectiveness inference).

- **The opening hypothesis remains intuitive rhetoric rather than a sharply falsifiable mechanism statement.** “**early steps demand fine temporal resolution and object-level detail, while late steps demand only a coarse judgment**” (Introduction, line 62) is now properly labeled a hypothesis, and “**a single fixed pairing may fit some of them poorly**” is appropriately conditional. However, “demand,” “fine detail,” and “coarse judgment” have no operational definition in the written text, while the actual modes are a continuous state, micro transition readout, and macro predicate readout (line 64). The reader cannot identify what result would disconfirm the posited phase-specific mismatch apart from the broad terminal-outcome aggregate. **Correction:** tie the hypothesis to observable predeclared diagnostics—for example, phase-stratified pair selection and endpoint error by cascade regime—and define the expected failure mode of each fixed/one-axis comparator.

## LOW
