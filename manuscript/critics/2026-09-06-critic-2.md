# Critic 2 Review — 2026-09-06

**Scope.** This review covers only the current title, abstract, and written Section 1 Introduction in `manuscript/iclr2026/iclr2026_conference.tex` (lines 14 and 57–97). Sections 2–6 are not started and are not reviewed as prose.

## Assessment of the shortened opening

The four-sentence Paragraph 1 is substantially better paced than the previous, broader lineage opening. It is concise, avoids journalistic person-centered framing, gives Dreamer and TD-MPC their distinct control roles, and uses predictive representations as a bridge rather than an unrelated survey detour. It is also mostly non-redundant with Paragraph 2: P1 establishes landscape and stakes, whereas P2 identifies the fixed-target convention. The compression does, however, make one technically consequential overgeneralization: the paragraph treats all cited predictive-representation work as if it necessarily defines an action-conditioned temporal horizon. That claim is too broad for the citations as presented and weakens the very horizon--description distinction that the paper needs to make precise.

## Issues

### CRITICAL — The paper still has no completed primary endpoint or central comparison

**Location:** Abstract, l.58; Introduction P9, l.89; contributions, ll.92–96.

**Offending text:** “**The planned evaluation compares**”; “**the authorized comparative result remains [TODO: result]**”; “**The common final-state readout and shared coordinate decoder are not completed ... and the central comparison has not been executed**”; and “**They do not include a demonstrated empirical advantage**.”

The manuscript openly describes a protocol and implemented infrastructure, not a completed empirical paper. The proposed joint-controller comparison against the five named comparators cannot support the title’s implication or the central scientific question until the specified readout and terminal-outcome-accuracy evaluation are completed. This remains dispositive irrespective of improvements to Paragraph 1.

### HIGH — Paragraph 1 overstates the common temporal/action-conditioned structure of Dreamer, TD-MPC, JEPA, and DINO-WM

**Location:** Introduction P1, l.65.

**Offending text:** “**Complementary work learns predictive representations \citep{assran2023ijepa,bardes2024vjepa} that can support planning through an action-conditioned dynamics model \citep{zhou2025dinowm}. Across these objectives, a model must define how far ahead to predict and in what form to express the future.**”

This is elegant compression but imprecise. Dreamer and TD-MPC are action-conditioned latent control models; the cited JEPA work is predictive-representation learning, and the cited pair includes a non-temporal image JEPA alongside a video JEPA. A representation learner does not, merely by being predictive, necessarily define an action-conditioned rollout horizon. DINO-WM is introduced only as an example of planning with an action-conditioned dynamics model, then silently used to make a claim about all “these objectives.” The resulting bridge obscures whether the paper’s gap concerns temporal action-conditioned forecasting, fixed representation targets, or the combination. For a paper whose novelty rests on joint horizon--output-description selection, this distinction cannot be hand-waved.

### HIGH — The abstract’s foundational literature claim and result are visibly unfinished

**Location:** Abstract, l.58.

**Offending text:** “**many latent world models predict at a single fixed horizon and output description [TODO: cite]**” and “**[TODO: result]**.”

The first statement is the premise for the gap and needs evidence; the second advertises a result that is absent. These are not cosmetic markers in an abstract: together they state that both the literature basis and empirical payoff of the paper remain unresolved.

### HIGH — The readout axis is still not established as a consequential choice rather than a choice of reporting head

**Location:** Introduction P5–P6, ll.77–80; P8, l.86.

**Offending text:** “**each phase of such a cascade asks for different prediction granularity**”; “**output-description mode ... selects which supervised prediction target is queried and scored**”; “**A continuous latent always remains the sole rollout carrier**”; and “**common-compute protocol**.”

The manuscript says that phase-dependent detail is useful but never identifies a cost, information constraint, learning consequence, or inference consequence of requesting one description mode rather than another. Because the continuous carrier always remains, mode selection currently reads as choosing an output head to query and score after the substantive rollout has already happened. The promised compute-controlled comparison therefore has no motivated causal pathway: why should this selection improve a terminal prediction, and what resource trade-off makes the selection nontrivial? This unresolved issue predates the shortened opening and remains central.

### HIGH — Paragraph 3’s closest-work taxonomy is internally unstable, so its novelty exclusion is not demonstrated

**Location:** Introduction P3, l.71.

**Offending text:** “**Other lines vary the description side alone**” includes “**fixed hierarchical prediction across multiple time scales**” and “**fixed short-detailed versus long-simplified segments**,” before asserting “**None of these lines jointly treats horizon and output-description mode**.”

Work explicitly described through multiple time scales, or short-versus-long segments tied to detailed-versus-simplified outputs, cannot simply be filed as description-only without a sharper distinction. The intended distinction may be fixed scheduling versus state-dependent joint requests, but the text does not articulate it before making a universal negative. This remains a direct threat to the claimed narrow gap.

### MEDIUM — The shortened opening reaches the abstract question before it supplies the setting that makes heterogeneous decisions meaningful

**Location:** Introduction P1–P2, ll.65–68; P5, l.77.

**Offending text:** P1 concludes “**a model must define how far ahead to predict and in what form to express the future**,” and P2 asks about “**heterogeneous decisions**”; only P5 provides “**actions occur infrequently, while their physical effects continue autonomously for many fixed simulation steps**.”

The new P1 is sufficient to introduce the question, but not sufficient to motivate why a single world model faces heterogeneous decision demands. Until P5, “different decisions” is an unsupported abstraction. The reader is asked to care about joint selection before seeing the collision-to-settling cascade that supplies the paper’s only concrete reason for it. This is a narrative-order problem, not a reason to restore the removed historical material.

### LOW — The P1-to-P2 handoff is clean but slightly repeats the same claim without adding immediate evidence

**Location:** Introduction P1–P2, ll.65–68.

**Offending text:** “**define how far ahead to predict and in what form**,” followed by “**Existing methods reasonably fix predictive horizons and targets**.”

This is not serious redundancy: P1 gives the conceptual bridge and P2 gives the literature-supported convention. Still, the second sentence of P2 must carry the burden of explaining why the first sentence matters; at present its “Different decisions” assertion is deferred rather than justified. The compressed opening itself should not be expanded merely to duplicate P2’s citations.

### LOW — The title presents an established method despite the manuscript’s admitted protocol-only status

**Location:** Title, l.14; Introduction P9, l.89.

**Offending text:** “**World Models with Joint Horizon--Description Selection**,” versus “**the central comparison has not been executed**.”

The title promises a method-level result, while the body limits the work to implementation and a prospective evaluation. This is a maturity mismatch, reinforced by the absent abstract result.

## What works in this revision

- **P1, l.65:** “**Dreamer learns behaviors from latent imagined rollouts ... while TD-MPC plans with learned latent dynamics and value estimates**” is a concise, materially accurate functional distinction. It avoids a literature roll call.
- **P1, l.65:** Removing the older historical exemplar improves reader onboarding. Four sentences are enough to establish a control/predictive-representation landscape without name-dropping or a journalistic lineage narrative.
- **P2, l.68:** “**Existing methods reasonably fix predictive horizons and targets to suit their intended objectives and downstream uses**” is appropriately respectful. “**A widespread default, not a universal rule**” properly narrows the claim.
- **P1–P2, ll.65–68:** The paragraphs are not materially redundant: P1 asks what models predict; P2 explains the conventional fixed prediction contract. The transition is natural once the technical scope of the P1 final sentence is corrected.

## Verdict: REJECT

**Single most damaging issue:** The manuscript states that its common final-state readout is incomplete and its central comparison has not been executed (P9, l.89). It therefore cannot establish the claimed value of joint horizon--description selection.
