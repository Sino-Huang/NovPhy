## HIGH

- **The zero-success matrix is described without the uncertainty needed to make its evidential strength legible.** `research_evidence.md` §Verified, bounded implementation evidence, line 24, states “**Each system recorded `0/15` successes, yielding a complete zero-success floor**”; `CONTEXT.md` §Issue-57 held-out gameplay evidence, line 129, and `content_brief.md` §Experiments, line 21, repeat this framing. The documents correctly prohibit equivalence and efficacy inferences, but “complete” and “floor” still invite a reader to overread 15 trials per system as a precise null. For a system with 0/15, the one-sided 95% binomial upper bound is roughly 18% success probability; the 75 trials cannot be pooled into a claim about any individual system. The written evidence summary must make that per-system uncertainty explicit (and identify the five systems in the reader-facing summary or directly point to their result rows) if it expects the null observation to carry any argumentative weight. Otherwise the only defensible takeaway is an underpowered observed failure count, not a substantively informative gameplay finding.

- **“Equal scores” is a metric-free null claim, so its scope cannot be audited from the manuscript-facing record.** `research_evidence.md` line 22 says the controllers “**have equal scores, with no observed advantage for the joint controller**”; `CONTEXT.md` lines 79–82 repeats “**equal scores in bounded model selection**”; and `content_brief.md` line 21 repeats the conclusion while providing only a single aggregate endpoint-violation rate. None of these passages names the score being equal, gives controller-wise values, states whether this was across seeds or a single realization, or supplies uncertainty. “No observed advantage” is acceptable only as a narrowly defined measured null; here the reader cannot tell what was measured or whether equality is exact, rounded, or statistically meaningless. Reporting the actual selection objective, both controllers’ values, evaluation units/seeds, and uncertainty is required before this can be used even as bounded negative evidence. It absolutely cannot serve as rhetorical support for the blocked central result.

The reader-facing account should distinguish enforced input/provenance invariants from demonstrated model behavior; no inference about deployment performance, temporal prediction quality, or controller validity follows from merge `6119b6c`.
