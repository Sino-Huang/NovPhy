\begin{abstract}
Physical dynamics unfold at different temporal and descriptive scales, yet latent world models typically predict using a fixed horizon and representation. We study whether prediction granularity should instead be selected at each decision point. We formulate granularity as a joint choice of prediction horizon and description mode, spanning continuous, micro-level, and macro-level predictions. Our framework uses a continuous latent carrier, a joint pair controller, and deployment-aligned temporal observations while preserving trajectory-level data isolation. The planned evaluation compares joint selection against fixed, single-axis, independent-axis, and parameter-matched factorized alternatives under a common-compute protocol in action-sparse environments with persistent physical effects. The primary question is whether coupling temporal and descriptive granularity improves terminal-outcome prediction under this protocol. Final comparative results remain pending authorization and completion of the sealed evaluation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Prediction granularity should be chosen at each decision point, not fixed when a world model is trained. This paper studies that choice in \emph{action-sparse persistent-effect environments}: settings in which actions occur infrequently while their physical effects continue autonomously for many fixed simulation steps. A single recorded intervention can set off a cascade that moves through collision onset and active rearrangement before settling. Each regime of the cascade asks something different of a predictive model. Early steps reward fine temporal resolution and object-level detail. Late steps reward only a coarse judgment, such as whether the structure has stabilized. We call this tension \emph{granularity mismatch}: a model locked to one prediction horizon and one output description must serve regimes whose predictive demands differ, and it cannot be well matched to all of them at once.

Two request parameters define granularity, and conflating them obscures the problem. The \emph{requested horizon} is the declared number of fixed steps that one prediction decision asks the world model to advance. It is a request made before prediction, distinct from the effective horizon that terminal clamping can produce during scoring. The \emph{description mode} is the requested description of the prediction itself. We consider three modes: a \emph{continuous} latent state, a \emph{micro} description of fine-grained transitions, and a \emph{macro} description of coarse state or event predicates. Horizon controls how far ahead the model looks. Mode controls what form the answer takes.

Many latent world models commit to a single prediction horizon and a single output representation at design time [TODO: cite]. Temporal abstraction in reinforcement learning instead varies how long an action is repeated or when the agent next acts [TODO: cite]. That line of work changes the agent's control schedule rather than the prediction problem posed to a model. Whether a world model should decide how far to predict, and at what descriptive granularity, as one coupled per-decision choice remains open.

We formulate granularity selection as one coupled decision. At each prediction point, a controller selects a single pair $(\Delta, \alpha)$, where $\Delta \in \{1, 5, 15\}$ is the requested horizon in fixed steps and $\alpha \in \{\mathrm{continuous}, \mathrm{micro}, \mathrm{macro}\}$ is the requested description mode. The nine pairs define the evaluation surface of this study. Whichever pair is chosen, a continuous predictive latent remains the sole carrier of the rollout between decisions. Micro and macro outputs are readouts over that latent, never recurrent state. The issue-60 deployment temporal carrier supplies the aligned agent-observable context for these predictions under role-isolated scenario lineages, with canonical engine state excluded from model and planner input outside declared supervision or alignment diagnosis. That carrier is implemented method infrastructure, not an empirical result.

We instantiate the study in the published NovPhy benchmark, a physical-reasoning benchmark for open-world AI systems [TODO: cite]. NovPhy supplies the setting only, and no published NovPhy finding is a result of the present program. The program itself, BG-NS-JEPA, is an in-progress latent world-model effort whose joint-pair controller is implemented and evaluated at bounded model-selection scope. Nothing in this paper should be read as a demonstrated advantage for that controller.

The central question is whether a learned joint-pair controller improves terminal-outcome prediction relative to five named comparators under one predeclared common-compute protocol. The first four comparators are controller-free policies: \emph{fixed-pair} holds one horizon--mode pair throughout, \emph{temporal-only} adapts only the horizon, \emph{description-only} adapts only the mode, and \emph{independent-axes} adapts both axes through separate decisions. The fifth comparator is a parameter-matched two-head controller that factorizes the joint choice across two heads. The specified primary endpoint is terminal-outcome accuracy, the accuracy of predicting the engine-defined final consequence of one recorded intervention, measured through a common final-state readout applied identically to every compared policy. That readout and its shared coordinate decoder are specified but not completed.

The final test of this question is sealed and awaits authorization. Six final-evaluation rollouts have been collected and sealed. Derivation and consumption of any final-evaluation metric are blocked pending final-evaluation authorization, and completion of a cohort release does not grant that authorization. The central comparative result remains [TODO: result].

Three bounded artifacts exist, and none answers the central question. The issue-10 model-selection comparison evaluated a distilled joint-pair controller and the parameter-matched two-head controller on 1,600 states with agent-observable inputs and oracle engine state excluded. The two controllers received equal scores, and no advantage was observed for either. These artifacts are not compute-matched to the issue-9 controller-free policy measurements. The issue-57 held-out gameplay study ran five systems on five held-out levels with three seeds each, for 75 trials. Every system recorded zero successes in its 15 trials, and the adaptive CEM/MPC planner requested the continuous mode at the longest horizon for all 44 recorded decisions, so adaptive granularity was not materially exercised. This zero-success floor establishes neither efficacy nor inefficacy of the controller, and it does not answer the central comparison. The issue-11 artifact is one aggregation round over six rollouts and 109 controller decisions using aligned ground-truth-expert carrier continuation, with zero deltas against an oracle-state baseline. It is not a model closed-loop rollout, and it does not measure terminal outcomes. Each artifact is valid within its recorded scope. None compares the joint-pair controller with the five comparators under the authorized protocol.

The contributions of this paper are a formulation, an implemented mechanism with its infrastructure, and a prospective protocol. They do not include a demonstrated empirical advantage.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Formulation.} We define action-sparse persistent-effect environments and granularity mismatch, separate the requested horizon from the description mode, and cast granularity as a joint per-decision choice over nine horizon--mode pairs in which a continuous latent is the sole rollout carrier.
  \item \textbf{Mechanism and infrastructure.} We specify the joint-pair controller, its parameter-matched two-head factorization, and the deployment-aligned temporal carrier that enforces agent-observable inputs and role-isolated scenario lineages. The carrier is infrastructure, not evidence.
  \item \textbf{Protocol.} We predeclare a common-compute comparison of the joint-pair controller against the four controller-free policies and the two-head controller, with terminal-outcome accuracy through a common final-state readout as the specified primary endpoint and six sealed final-evaluation rollouts awaiting authorization.
\end{itemize}
