\begin{abstract}
Physical dynamics unfold at different temporal and descriptive scales, yet many latent world models predict at a single fixed horizon and output description [TODO: cite]. We ask whether prediction granularity should instead be selected at each decision point in action-sparse environments with persistent physical effects. We formulate granularity as a joint choice of prediction horizon and output-description mode, spanning continuous, micro-level, and macro-level readouts over a continuous latent carrier that remains the sole rollout state. The framework pairs this carrier with a joint pair controller and deployment-aligned temporal observations while preserving trajectory-level data isolation. The planned evaluation compares joint selection against four controller-free policies (fixed-pair, temporal-only, description-only, and independent-axes) and one parameter-matched two-head controller under a predeclared common-compute protocol. The primary question is whether coupling temporal and descriptive granularity improves terminal-outcome prediction under this protocol. Six final-evaluation rollouts are collected and sealed, and the authorized comparative result remains [TODO: result].
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

This paper asks whether a world model should choose its prediction granularity at each decision point rather than fix it at design time. We study that question in \emph{action-sparse persistent-effect environments}: settings in which actions occur infrequently while their physical effects continue autonomously for many fixed simulation steps. A single recorded intervention can set off a cascade that moves through collision onset and active rearrangement before settling. We hypothesize that each regime of the cascade asks something different of a predictive model: early steps demand fine temporal resolution and object-level detail, while late steps demand only a coarse judgment, such as whether the structure has stabilized. We call this hypothesized tension \emph{granularity mismatch}. A model locked to one prediction horizon and one output description serves all regimes at once, and a single fixed pairing may fit some of them poorly. Whether this mismatch harms prediction in practice is part of what the planned comparison must test.

Two request parameters define granularity, and conflating them obscures the problem. The \emph{requested horizon} is the declared number of fixed steps that one prediction decision asks the world model to advance. It is a request made before prediction, distinct from the effective horizon that terminal clamping can produce during scoring. The \emph{description mode} is the requested output description of that prediction: a \emph{continuous} latent state, a \emph{micro} readout of fine-grained transitions, or a \emph{macro} readout of coarse state or event predicates. Horizon controls how far ahead the model looks. Mode selects which readout of the carrier the prediction returns, not the state that carries the rollout.

Many latent world models commit to a single prediction horizon and a single output description at design time [TODO: cite]. Temporal abstraction in reinforcement learning instead varies how long an action is repeated or when the agent next acts [TODO: cite]. That line of work changes the agent's control schedule rather than the prediction problem posed to a model. Whether coupled per-decision selection of horizon and output description improves prediction in this setting is the gap this paper proposes to test.

We formulate granularity selection as one coupled decision. At each prediction point, a controller selects a single pair $(\Delta, \alpha)$, where $\Delta \in \{1, 5, 15\}$ is the requested horizon in fixed steps and $\alpha \in \{\mathrm{continuous}, \mathrm{micro}, \mathrm{macro}\}$ is the requested description mode. The nine pairs define the evaluation surface of this study. Whichever pair is chosen, a continuous predictive latent remains the sole carrier of the rollout between decisions. Micro and macro outputs are readouts over that latent, never recurrent state. The model observes only agent-available inputs, and training, model-selection, and evaluation data are kept separate under declared roles. Companion tooling implements the retraining manifests, matched carrier-alignment checks, and evaluation harnesses used to assemble the comparator systems. Both the carrier pipeline and this tooling are implemented infrastructure, not empirical evidence.

We instantiate the study in the published NovPhy benchmark, a physical-reasoning benchmark for open-world AI systems [TODO: cite]. NovPhy supplies the setting only, and no published NovPhy finding is a result of the present program. The program itself, BG-NS-JEPA, is an in-progress latent world-model effort whose joint-pair controller is implemented and evaluated at bounded model-selection scope. Nothing in this paper should be read as a demonstrated advantage for that controller.

The central question is whether a learned joint-pair controller improves terminal-outcome prediction relative to five named comparators under one predeclared common-compute protocol. The first four comparators are controller-free policies: \emph{fixed-pair} holds one horizon--mode pair throughout, \emph{temporal-only} adapts only the horizon, \emph{description-only} adapts only the mode, and \emph{independent-axes} adapts both axes through separate decisions. The fifth comparator is a parameter-matched two-head controller that factorizes the joint choice across two heads. The specified primary endpoint is terminal-outcome accuracy, the accuracy of predicting the engine-defined final consequence of one recorded intervention, measured through a common final-state readout applied identically to every compared policy. That readout and its shared coordinate decoder are specified but not completed.

Six final-evaluation rollouts for this test are collected and sealed. Several prerequisites remain incomplete. The common final-state readout and shared coordinate decoder are not finished. Derivation and consumption of any final-evaluation metric are blocked pending final-evaluation authorization. The comparators have not been executed under the predeclared protocol. The prespecified replicate analysis, the reproducible archive, the consolidated report, and the terminal program audit also remain open. The central comparative result remains [TODO: result].

Existing evidence is bounded, and none of it answers the central question. A bounded model-selection comparison of the distilled joint-pair controller and the parameter-matched two-head controller produced equal scores with no observed advantage for either, and a frozen held-out gameplay matrix recorded zero successes for every evaluated system while its adaptive planner collapsed to a single horizon--mode pair on every decision, so adaptive granularity was not materially exercised. Neither artifact compares the joint-pair controller with the five comparators under the authorized protocol.

The contributions of this paper are a formulation, an implemented mechanism with its tooling, and a prospective protocol. They do not include a demonstrated empirical advantage.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Formulation.} We define action-sparse persistent-effect environments and granularity mismatch, separate the requested horizon from the output-description mode, and cast granularity as a joint per-decision choice over nine horizon--mode pairs in which a continuous latent is the sole rollout carrier.
  \item \textbf{Mechanism and tooling.} We specify the joint-pair controller and its parameter-matched two-head factorization, together with the implemented carrier pipeline that isolates model inputs from privileged engine state and the matched retraining and evaluation tooling used to assemble the comparator systems. The carrier and tooling are infrastructure, not evidence.
  \item \textbf{Protocol.} We predeclare a common-compute comparison of the joint-pair controller against the four controller-free policies and the two-head controller, with terminal-outcome accuracy through a common final-state readout as the specified primary endpoint. Six final-evaluation rollouts are collected and sealed, while the common readout, the authorized metric derivation, and the comparator execution remain incomplete.
\end{itemize}
