\begin{abstract}
Physical dynamics unfold at different temporal and descriptive scales, yet many latent world models predict at a single fixed horizon and output description [TODO: cite]. We ask whether prediction granularity should instead be selected at each decision point in action-sparse environments with persistent physical effects. We formulate granularity as a joint choice of prediction horizon and output-description mode, spanning continuous, micro-level, and macro-level readouts over a continuous latent carrier that remains the sole rollout state. The framework pairs this carrier with a joint pair controller and deployment-aligned temporal observations while preserving trajectory-level data isolation. The planned evaluation compares joint selection against four controller-free policies (fixed-pair, temporal-only, description-only, and independent-axes) and one parameter-matched two-head controller under a predeclared common-compute protocol. The primary question is whether coupling temporal and descriptive granularity improves terminal-outcome prediction under this protocol. \section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

% Paragraph 1: Establish world models as predictors of action consequences used for planning and control in imagination.
World models let an agent predict the consequences of its actions and act on those predictions through planning or imagined learning. \citet{ha2018recurrent} showed that a learned generative model of an environment can carry policy evolution entirely inside the model. The Dreamer line learns latent dynamics and derives behaviors from imagined rollouts \citep{hafner2020dreamer}, a design that extends to diverse control tasks \citep{hafner2025dreamerv3}. \citet{hansen2022tdmpc} combine a learned latent model with local trajectory optimization and a terminal value estimate for model-predictive control. Across these systems, prediction quality is the substrate on which control rests.

% Paragraph 2: State the conventional prediction contract, fixed temporal targets or recursive rollout, without claiming universality.
These systems share a convention about what a prediction request is. Latent-dynamics control methods conventionally fix their temporal targets or planning horizons, reaching further ahead through recursive one-step rollout with value bootstrapping \citep{hafner2020dreamer,hansen2022tdmpc,sobal2025pldm}. Joint-embedding approaches fix the predictive representation and target at training time \citep{assran2023ijepa,bardes2024vjepa}, and recent latent world models built on frozen or end-to-end features inherit that fixed target \citep{zhou2025dinowm,maes2026leworldmodel}. Under this convention, the granularity of a prediction, meaning how far ahead the model looks and in what form the answer returns, is a design-time constant rather than a per-decision choice. The convention is a widespread default, not a universal rule.

% Paragraph 3: Acknowledge nearest single-axis relaxations and state the narrow joint gap.
Recent work relaxes one dimension of this contract at a time. Variable-length latent world models vary the temporal offset of each prediction \citep{du2026vlwm}, and surprise signals can set hierarchical chunk boundaries in open-loop video prediction \citep{iiyama2026sunta}. Reliability estimates can adapt rollout depth during imagination \citep{hafez2020improving}. Other lines vary the description side alone: state-dependent scene-representation resolution under a fixed planning horizon \citep{wang2023dynamic}, fixed hierarchical prediction across multiple time scales \citep{shaj2023multi,villarcorrales2022mspred}, per-frame abstraction of action forecasts outside a planning world model \citep{scarafoni2022islands}, and fixed short-detailed versus long-simplified segments within one model-predictive controller \citep{brudigam2021mpc}. \citet{wang2026adajepa} instead adapt model parameters at test time, which is not granularity selection. None of these lines jointly treats horizon and output-description mode as one state-dependent prediction request issued to an action-conditioned world model. That joint treatment is the narrow gap this paper targets.

% Paragraph 4: Distinguish control-schedule temporal abstraction from prediction granularity.
Temporal abstraction in reinforcement learning answers a different question. Options and their learned variants decide when and for how long the agent acts \citep{sutton1999options,bacon2017optioncritic}, and \citet{biedenkapp2021temporl} learn when to commit to an action repetition. These methods adapt the agent's control schedule, not what the world model predicts or how far ahead the prediction extends. Our question sits on the prediction side of that boundary.

% Paragraph 5: Introduce the action-sparse persistent-effect setting with physics benchmarks, mismatch as hypothesis.
The setting that motivates our question is the \emph{action-sparse persistent-effect environment}: actions occur infrequently, while their physical effects continue autonomously for many fixed simulation steps. A single intervention can set off a cascade that moves through collision onset and active rearrangement before settling. Physical-reasoning benchmarks supply concrete instances of such dynamics, from task-oriented physical puzzles \citep{bakhtin2019phyre} to video-based physical prediction and reasoning suites \citep{bear2021physion,yi2020clevrer}. We build on NovPhy, a published benchmark that evaluates how agents detect and adapt to novel physical dynamics across scenarios \citep{pinto2024novphy}. We hypothesize that each phase of such a cascade asks for different prediction granularity: fine temporal resolution and relation-level detail near the intervention, coarse state judgments near settling. We call this hypothesized tension \emph{granularity mismatch}. Whether it harms prediction in practice is an empirical question, not an assumption of this work.

% Paragraph 6: Define requested horizon and output-description mode operationally over the 3 by 3 surface.
We make granularity operational as two request parameters. The \emph{requested horizon} $\Delta \in \{1, 5, 15\}$ is the declared number of fixed simulation steps that one prediction decision asks the world model to advance. The \emph{output-description mode} $\alpha \in \{\mathrm{continuous}, \mathrm{micro}, \mathrm{macro}\}$ selects which supervised prediction target is queried and scored at that decision: \emph{continuous} exposes the predictive latent used for recursive continuation, \emph{micro} requests fine transition relations such as contact and support, and \emph{macro} requests coarse state or event predicates such as \texttt{steady-state} and \texttt{structure-unstable}. The nine $(\Delta, \alpha)$ pairs form the evaluation surface of this study. A continuous latent always remains the sole rollout carrier. Mode selection changes the readout requested over that carrier, never the recurrent state representation itself, and it implies no switch in spatial resolution or between pixel-level and object-level representations.

% Paragraph 7: Present the proposed controller and carrier, separating published NovPhy from in-progress BG-NS-JEPA.
Our design pairs this request space with a learned joint-pair controller that selects one $(\Delta, \alpha)$ pair per prediction decision from agent-observable inputs. A deployment-aligned temporal carrier supplies the model with agent observations only, keeps training, model-selection, and evaluation data separate under declared roles, and carries every rollout between decisions. The carrier and the matched training and evaluation tooling around it are implemented infrastructure, not empirical evidence of any benefit. Two names need crisp separation. NovPhy is the published benchmark and supplies the setting \citep{pinto2024novphy}. BG-NS-JEPA is the in-progress world-model program built on that setting, and no published NovPhy finding is a result of this program.

% Paragraph 8: State the falsifiable comparison, exact comparators, specified endpoint, and frozen compute accounting.
The central question is whether the learned joint-pair controller improves terminal-outcome prediction relative to five comparators under one predeclared common-compute protocol. Four comparators are controller-free policies: \emph{fixed-pair} holds one horizon--mode pair throughout, \emph{temporal-only} adapts only the horizon, \emph{description-only} adapts only the mode, and \emph{independent-axes} chooses the two axes through separate decisions. The fifth is a parameter-matched two-head controller that factorizes the joint choice across two heads. The specified primary endpoint is terminal-outcome accuracy, the accuracy of predicting the engine-defined final consequence of one recorded intervention through a common final-state readout applied identically to every compared policy. The frozen protocol restricts evaluation to two prospectively calibrated compute-support budgets, and any configuration exceeding its budget is ineligible. It reports policy compute per simulated frame and full end-to-end compute per simulated frame for every system, and it pairs effects within six complete rollouts.

% Contribution list: frame the paper as formulation, implemented controller design and infrastructure, and prospective protocol, with no empirical advantage claim.
The contributions of this paper are a formulation, an implemented controller design with its supporting infrastructure, and a prospective protocol. They do not include a demonstrated empirical advantage.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Formulation.} We define action-sparse persistent-effect environments and the granularity-mismatch hypothesis, and we cast prediction granularity as one joint per-decision request over nine horizon--mode pairs carried by a single continuous latent.
  \item \textbf{Implemented controller design and infrastructure.} We implement the joint-pair controller, its parameter-matched two-head factorization, the agent-observable deployment-aligned temporal carrier, and the matched training and evaluation tooling that assembles the comparator systems. This infrastructure is implemented and exercised at bounded model-selection scope. It is not evidence of benefit.
  \item \textbf{Prospective protocol.} We predeclare a common-compute comparison against fixed-pair, temporal-only, description-only, independent-axes, and the two-head controller, with terminal-outcome accuracy through a common final-state readout as the specified primary endpoint over six collected and sealed rollouts.
\end{itemize}
