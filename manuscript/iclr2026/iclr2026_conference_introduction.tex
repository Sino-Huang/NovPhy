% Paragraph 1 (#91-WP5 compressed): world models, the ranking use, the setting, the benchmark.
A world model approximates an environment's transition dynamics, conditioned on the current state and a candidate action. Dreamer learns behaviors from latent imagined rollouts \citep{hafner2020dreamer,hafner2025dreamerv3}, and TD-MPC plans with a learned latent model \citep{hansen2022tdmpc}. We study the planning use, in which the model ranks candidate actions, in an \emph{action-sparse persistent-effect environment}: one intervention sets off a cascade of collisions before settling. Our testbed is NovPhy \citep{pinto2024novphy}, a physical-reasoning benchmark \citep{bakhtin2019phyre,bear2021physion,yi2020clevrer}. Appendix~\ref{sec:app:related} discusses fixed-granularity designs.


% Paragraph 2 (#91-WP5 compressed from former ¶6-¶7): the granularity surface and the program split.
The program began with a granularity question. One action-conditioned model could select, per decision, a horizon $\Delta \in \{1, 5, 15\}$ and an output-description mode $\alpha \in \{\mathrm{continuous}, \mathrm{micro}, \mathrm{macro}\}$ (Section~\ref{sec:mth:setting}). BG-NS-JEPA is the in-progress program behind this $(\Delta, \alpha)$ surface, and no published NovPhy finding is its result. The paper's primary evidence is a closed-loop audit of its fixed-horizon rankers (Section~\ref{sec:syn:picture}), and the learned controller never entered the closed-loop record.

We first tested the question as posed. Under a frozen prospective protocol with six complete sealed rollouts (Section~\ref{sec:mth:protocol}), joint selection showed zero paired gain at the lower compute-support budget and higher endpoint error than a fixed pair at the higher budget (paired gain $-5.2 \times 10^{-4}$, interval $[-7.1, -3.6] \times 10^{-4}$), so the frozen rule returned \emph{not\_supported\_by\_this\_experiment} (Appendix~\ref{sec:app:confirmatory}). On the new rolling and sliding families, at four held-out states, we then read a $+0.0887$ $[+0.0155, +0.2118]$ $h{=}1$ ranking-regret contrast as support for adaptive granularity. Its per-seed values are $0$, $0$, and $+0.2662$, so one of three seeds carries it. % issue-77-n1-diagnostic-v1/findings.md; #15 data/runtime_evidence/issue-15/
All of these contrasts are scored against a replay-cost proxy read at the right-censored $t{=}600$ end of window, and auditing that proxy invalidated it. Its success predicate agreed with replay engine evidence on only $0.1299$ of cells against a pre-declared $0.50$ floor, and on a verified engine-event channel the proxy over-called success by $+0.8958$ $[+0.6875, +1.0000]$. % issue-82-zero-floor-oracle-v1/findings.md; issue-85-engine-outcome-reactive-v1/findings.md; r6: h=15, 0/45 pilot and 0.1064 agreement cut (kept in §5/§7)
We therefore treat the proxy-scored anatomy of Section~\ref{sec:exp:anatomy} as heuristic and record the $+0.0887$ contrast as unsupported: carried by one seed on an invalidated proxy. Proxy-scored closed-loop claims in this setting should be read as provisional until checked against engine-truth outcomes~\citep{henderson2018deep,agarwal2021deep}, as closed-loop audits have overturned open-loop impressions elsewhere~\citep{zhang2026worldinworld}.

The engine-truth channel then became the measurement. An \emph{oracle pass} executed every candidate of the original 13-candidate sweep on each development N1 state and read the engine's own removal events. A \emph{completion pass} re-executed the slots it left unmeasured, a \emph{timeout audit} checked whether that missingness depended on outcome, and a \emph{restatement} rescored selection at the source-member unit. Over 15 source members, the completed ceiling finds a successful single-shot action for 7 of 14 measurable members ($0.5000$, descriptive 95\% interval $[0.2143, 0.7857]$), with exactly one successful candidate per ceiling state. Two further inventories over the same members, the offset sweep and the drag grid, give ceilings of 9 of 15 and 14 of 15 members, so the ceiling is relative to the inventory. % issue-92-selection-validity-v1/findings.md §2, §6; summary.json chance_references.member_terms (k=1 for all 7); .local-artifacts/issue-93-second-parameterization-v1/comparisons.csv offset_ceiling, grid_ceiling; r6: 24 states / 9 duplicate pairs left to Table 1
On this frozen N1 membership, no frozen model ranker selected a successful candidate in its 108 ceiling-state cells (plus 36 cells of a no-model ordinal prior, also without a success; accounting in Table~\ref{tab:syn:oracle}). % issue-92-selection-validity-v1/findings.md §6
The literal zero is specific to the N1 membership. On a broader type010101 evaluation split with 12 systems, the frozen zero-shot arm selected engine-truth successes in $13/612 = 0.0212$ cells against inventory-matched chance $0.0407$. The adapted few-shot arm, reported separately and not frozen-system evidence, reached $29/612 = 0.0474$ (paired difference $+0.0067$ $[-0.0202, +0.0368]$), with hits localized to 5 of 17 states. % issue-92-cross-pool-audit-v1/findings.md Q2; few-shot paired: .local-artifacts/issue-92-cross-pool-audit-v1/comparisons.csv:10; r6: 2/153 analogue clause cut (appendix Table 17)
The zero holds on both sides of the exposure split: the two ceiling members never seen in training score 0/36 at top-1 (AUC $0.3426$), and the five exposed ones score 0/72 (AUC $0.4838$). Across 16 decision-only lookahead arms, 15 select no success in 36 ceiling cells and \texttt{hybrid-fixed-h15-e225} selects 2/36 ($[0.0000, 0.1429]$), so the universal statement above binds only to the primary record of three frozen model rankers. % summary.json exposure_split; issue-92-lookahead-control-v1/findings.md; r6: second-seed sentence cut (abstract, Table 1, §7)

We contribute the following.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{An inventory-relative, per-state engine ceiling used as the denominator.} Measured on three inventories over the same 15 members ($7/14$, $9/15$, $14/15$) and a type010101 breadth pool ($25/46$), it makes a zero success rate read as a selection measurement. PHYRE's AUCCESS and its OPTIMAL oracle ranking are the precedent \citep[\S3.2, \S4.4]{bakhtin2019phyre} (Appendix~\ref{sec:app:rel:eval}). % .local-artifacts/issue-92-selection-validity-v1/summary.json member_ceiling; issue-93-second-parameterization-v1/comparisons.csv; issue-92-cross-pool-audit-v1/findings.md Q1; PHYRE arXiv:1908.05656 §3.2, §4.4 Fig. 4
  \item \textbf{Pooled top-1 at or below chance under that ceiling on every frozen record, with controls and a bounding counterexample.} It holds on the original sweep, the offset sweep, the drag grid, and the held-out zero-shot arm, under 16 lookahead arms, a second engine seed, an exposure split, an inverted ranker, and a constant-policy exhibit. The adapted few-shot arm ($29/612$, hits on 5 of 17 states) bounds it (Table~\ref{tab:syn:oracle}).
  \item \textbf{Our post-hoc ordering headline failed its pre-registered replication, and the audit chain exposed it.} Ordering AUC moves across chance with the inventory while pooled top-1 does not, so ordering and selection must be reported separately against a measured ceiling. Frozen plans, typed failures, verbatim tokens, and validation commands for every artifact root are released (Appendix~\ref{sec:app:repro}).
\end{itemize}

% ===== Significance close (r6): delta vs Lambert/Girdhar = mismatch between two decision metrics under a measured ceiling.
Closed-loop world-model evaluations such as World-in-World report task success without a per-state solvability ceiling \citep{zhang2026worldinworld}, which leaves a zero ambiguous between hard states and bad choices. Objective-mismatch work, including forward-predictor ranking of PHYRE candidates, shows that prediction quality need not track task success \citep{lambert2020objective,girdhar2020forward}. We show a mismatch between two decision metrics under a measured per-state ceiling: ordering AUC moves with the inventory while pooled top-1 does not. The lesson is to verify the outcome channel and report top-1 against a measured per-state ceiling beside any ordering statistic. % r4 I25: Girdhar arXiv:2006.10734 §5.1-5.2
