% Paragraph 1 (#91-WP5 compressed): world models, the ranking use, the setting, the benchmark.
A world model approximates an environment's transition dynamics, conditioned on the current state and a candidate action. Dreamer learns behaviors from latent imagined rollouts \citep{hafner2020dreamer,hafner2025dreamerv3}, and TD-MPC plans with a learned latent model \citep{hansen2022tdmpc}. We study the planning use, in which the model ranks candidate actions, in an \emph{action-sparse persistent-effect environment}: one intervention sets off a cascade of collisions before settling. Our testbed is NovPhy \citep{pinto2024novphy}, a physical-reasoning benchmark \citep{bakhtin2019phyre,bear2021physion,yi2020clevrer}. Appendix~\ref{sec:app:related} discusses fixed-granularity designs.


% Paragraph 2 (#91-WP5 compressed from former ¶6-¶7): the granularity surface and the program split.
The program began with a granularity question. One action-conditioned model could select, per decision, a horizon $\Delta \in \{1, 5, 15\}$ and an output-description mode $\alpha \in \{\mathrm{continuous}, \mathrm{micro}, \mathrm{macro}\}$ (Section~\ref{sec:mth:setting}). BG-NS-JEPA is the in-progress program behind this surface, and no published NovPhy finding is its result. The paper's primary evidence is a closed-loop audit of its fixed-horizon rankers (Section~\ref{sec:syn:picture}), and the learned controller never entered the closed-loop record.

We first tested the question as posed. Under a frozen prospective protocol with six complete sealed rollouts (Section~\ref{sec:mth:protocol}), joint selection showed zero paired gain at the lower compute-support budget and higher endpoint error than a fixed pair at the higher budget (paired gain $-5.2 \times 10^{-4}$, interval $[-7.1, -3.6] \times 10^{-4}$), so the frozen rule returned \emph{not\_supported\_by\_this\_experiment} (Appendix~\ref{sec:app:confirmatory}). On the new rolling and sliding families, at four held-out states, we then read a $+0.0887$ $[+0.0155, +0.2118]$ $h{=}1$ ranking-regret contrast as support for adaptive granularity. Its per-seed values are $0$, $0$, and $+0.2662$, so one of three seeds carries it. % issue-77-n1-diagnostic-v1/findings.md; #15 data/runtime_evidence/issue-15/
The macro symbolic-execution contrast at $h{=}15$ has the same shape ($+0.0944$, per-seed $+0.2905$, $-0.0215$, $+0.0141$). At $h{=}15$ the training contrast is null on the new families ($-0.0051$ $[-0.1625, +0.1373]$) and exactly zero on the original families ($0.000000$ $[0, 0]$). % issue-77-n1-diagnostic-v1/findings.md; data/issue-76-closeout/findings.md
All of these contrasts are scored against a replay-cost proxy read at the right-censored $t{=}600$ end of window, and auditing that proxy invalidated it in stages. A reactive closed-loop pilot stopped at its prevalence gate with no first-shot success in 45 valid executions. The proxy's success predicate then agreed with replay engine evidence on only $0.1299$ of cells against a pre-declared $0.50$ floor. On a verified engine-event channel, proxy--engine agreement was $0.1064$ and the proxy over-called success by $+0.8958$ $[+0.6875, +1.0000]$. % issue-80; issue-82-zero-floor-oracle-v1/findings.md; issue-85-engine-outcome-reactive-v1/findings.md
We therefore treat the proxy-scored anatomy of Section~\ref{sec:exp:anatomy} as a heuristic supporting section and record the $+0.0887$ contrast as unsupported: carried by one seed on an invalidated proxy. Proxy-scored closed-loop claims in this setting should be read as provisional until checked against engine-truth outcomes~\citep{henderson2018deep,agarwal2021deep}, as closed-loop audits have overturned open-loop impressions elsewhere~\citep{zhang2026worldinworld}.

The engine-truth channel then became the measurement, built in four stages named by function. An \emph{oracle pass} executed every candidate of the frozen 13-action inventory on each development N1 state and read the engine's own removal events. A \emph{completion pass} re-executed the slots the oracle pass left unmeasured, a \emph{timeout audit} checked whether that missingness depended on outcome, and a \emph{restatement} rescored selection at the source-member unit. Over 15 source members (24 states, including 9 byte-identical duplicate pairs), the completed ceiling finds a successful single-shot action for 7 of 14 measurable members ($0.5000$, descriptive 95\% interval $[0.2143, 0.7857]$), with exactly one successful candidate per ceiling state. % issue-92-selection-validity-v1/findings.md §2, §6; summary.json chance_references.member_terms (k=1 for all 7)
On this frozen N1 membership, no frozen model ranker selected a successful candidate in its 108 bound ceiling-state cells (plus 36 cells of a no-model ordinal prior, also without a success; accounting in Table~\ref{tab:syn:oracle}). % issue-92-selection-validity-v1/findings.md §6
The zero is membership-specific. On a broader type010101 evaluation split with 12 systems, the frozen zero-shot arm selected engine-truth successes in $13/612 = 0.0212$ cells against inventory-matched chance $0.0407$. The adapted few-shot arm, reported separately and not frozen-system evidence, reached $29/612 = 0.0474$. Hits localize to 5 of 17 states, and the four systems analogous to the N1 rankers score 2/153 in each condition. % issue-92-cross-pool-audit-v1/findings.md (Q2; #87-analogue 2/153 per condition)
The zero holds on both sides of the exposure split: the two ceiling members never seen in training score 0/36 at top-1 (AUC $0.3426$), and the five exposed ones score 0/72 (AUC $0.4838$). Across 16 decision-only lookahead arms, 15 select no success in 36 ceiling cells and \texttt{hybrid-fixed-h15-e225} selects 2/36 ($[0.0000, 0.1429]$), so the universal statement above binds only to the primary record of three frozen model rankers. A second engine seed leaves 26/26 re-executed verdicts unchanged, and 19 of the 26 decision frames are byte-identical, so the engine is effectively deterministic for these episodes. % summary.json exposure_split; issue-92-lookahead-control-v1/findings.md; issue-92-cross-seed-invariance-v1/findings.md §2 (19 of 26 byte-identical)

The failure localizes to candidate ranking, and AUC is its headline statistic. On N1, predicted-cost ordering places the successful action at or below chance, with AUC $0.4367$ $[0.4045, 0.4710]$ over 108 model cells and $0.4180$ $[0.3135, 0.5252]$ when clustered by member. On the type010101 split both cell-unit AUC point estimates sit below $0.5$: $0.2039$ $[0.0762, 0.3324]$ for the frozen zero-shot arm, held out from that split, and $0.2732$ $[0.2182, 0.3239]$ for the few-shot arm adapted on it (each over the 216 of 612 cells on the 6 states with a success, clustered by state). The inverted (maximum-cost) ranker selects the success in $15/612$ zero-shot and $21/612$ few-shot cells against the same chance $0.0407$ (paired $-0.0162$ $[-0.0392, +0.0026]$ and $-0.0064$ $[-0.0270, +0.0118]$, 17 state clusters, descriptive), so these records give no support to a cost-sign reclassification. % .local-artifacts/issue-92-cross-pool-audit-v1/summary.json cross_split.per_condition.{zero-shot,few-shot}.auc.cell_unit (units 216, clusters 6, draws 10000; scored_cells 216; cells 612); inverted: .local-artifacts/issue-91-inverted-ranker-v1/summary.json (per_condition)
Top-1 selection is 0/108 against chance $0.0780$. The hybrid ranker orders actions by the sweep coordinate (Spearman monotonicity $0.9986$), and the two continuous rankers less tightly ($0.4924$ and $0.6130$; pooled $0.8776$). On N1 this ordering puts every model choice among high-arc lobs (ordinals 7--12, where engine replay found no success in 70 candidate slots), while each ceiling state's single success is a flat or mid direct shot (ordinals 2--6). This separation is a descriptive observation on N1, and on type010101 the breadth pool's successes also sit at ordinals 0, 1, and 10--12. At a lookahead of 1 ($h{=}1$) or 25 ($h{=}5$) carrier frames per candidate, against engine outcomes that resolve 97.8--400.9 agent frames after the decision (between 0.25\% and 25.6\% of the outcome horizon; endpoint arms in Appendix~\ref{sec:app:lookahead}), selection therefore fails at within-state action discrimination along the candidate sweep. % issue-92-selection-validity-v1/summary.json ordering_mechanism (spearman drag_x per_system 0.4924/0.6130/0.9986, pooled 0.8776); lookahead .local-artifacts/issue-92-lookahead-control-v1/findings.md line 9; 0.25% and 25.6% DERIVED as 1/400.9 and 25/97.8 from findings.md lines 9 and 14

% ===== Spine (d): scope and limits. Sources as above.
The claim is narrow by construction. It covers single-shot, decision-only selection by three frozen fixed-horizon rankers on development N1 lineages, with 7 ceiling clusters and descriptive intervals, one primary engine seed plus a 26-slot second-seed check, and a candidate set that is a one-dimensional launch-angle sweep. The adaptive controller never appears in the closed-loop record, and no gameplay success is claimed.

% ===== Contribution list (#91 WP1).
We contribute the following.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A measured per-state action ceiling.} PHYRE certifies solvability by construction and scores agents by AUCCESS, a log-weighted area under the task-level success curve over up to 100 attempts \citep{bakhtin2019phyre}. We measure the ceiling in the engine for a frozen 13-candidate inventory, on the N1 membership ($7/14$ members) and a type010101 breadth pool ($25/46$), so a zero success rate reads as ranking failure on solvable states (Section~\ref{sec:syn:picture}). % PHYRE facts verified by Scout (arXiv:1908.05656 §3.1 tiers, §3.2 AUCCESS, §4.2 attempt budget); r4 I25: release clause dropped (§4.2 fn. 3 is future tense)
  \item \textbf{A scoped selection-validity measurement with controls.} Engine-truth AUC at or below chance on N1 and below $0.5$ in point estimate on a second split, with a 16-arm lookahead control, measured replay determinism, an exposure split, and the counterexample split that bounds the result (Table~\ref{tab:syn:oracle}).
  \item \textbf{An audit-chain protocol.} Frozen plans, typed failures, separated training, symbolic-execution, and adaptation estimands, and a sequence of audits in which each stage tests the measurement channel of the one before, released with validation commands for every cited artifact root (Appendix~\ref{sec:app:repro}).
\end{itemize}

% ===== Significance close (r2 I2/I7): positioned against closed-loop world-model success reporting, PHYRE as precedent.
Closed-loop world-model evaluations such as World-in-World report task success without a per-state solvability ceiling \citep{zhang2026worldinworld}, which leaves a zero ambiguous between hard states and bad choices. We supply a measured per-state ceiling and, under it, a per-candidate engine-truth AUC in a single-shot cascade regime, where one launch commits the outcome. Objective-mismatch work, including forward-predictor ranking of PHYRE candidates, shows that prediction quality need not track task performance \citep{lambert2020objective,girdhar2020forward}. We locate the mismatch within a state. For anyone evaluating action-conditioned models, the lesson is to verify the outcome channel and test within-state candidate ordering, rather than to treat aggregate prediction quality as evidence of good decisions. % r4 I25: Girdhar arXiv:2006.10734 §5.1-5.2
