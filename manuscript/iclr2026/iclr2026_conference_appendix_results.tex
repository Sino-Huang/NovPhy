\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\rev{Short-window and long-horizon training}}{Short-window and long-horizon training}}
\label{sec:app:res:rollout}

\begin{revblock}
\nopagebreak
The submitted model was trained with local losses on short windows only (recipe B in \#112). Adding the long-horizon recursive loss (recipe L) removes its $\Delta=1$ divergence. On the 12 development lineages scored leave-one-lineage-out (exploratory, not the sealed NovPhy split), the engine-referenced position MSE at $t=225$ of $F(1,\textsf{cont})$ falls from 3622 under B to 0.088 under L, with $L-B=-3622$ [$-4089$, $-3150$]. At $\Delta=5$ it falls from 0.303 to 0.041, and at $\Delta=15$ from 0.037 to 0.030. The primary recipe (LC) adds a decision-contrastive ranking loss to L (Section~\ref{sec:mth:model}). These numbers come from the \#112 report and were not rescored for this revision.
\end{revblock}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\rev{Request-code parameterization}}{Request-code parameterization}}
\label{sec:app:res:code}

\begin{revblock}
\nopagebreak
The predictor's request code is a fused MLP of the horizon and description embeddings (Section~\ref{sec:mth:model}). \#121 retrained the primary recipe with two alternatives: an additive code, which sums separate horizon and description terms, and a factored FiLM code. On the grid, the trained fused parameterization outperforms the trained additive parameterization on nine-fixed-request mean AUC: fused 0.722 [0.703, 0.741], additive 0.672 [0.653, 0.691], factored 0.684 [0.666, 0.701], with fused $-$ additive $=+0.050$ [$+0.041$, $+0.059$] and fused $-$ factored $=+0.052$ [$+0.049$, $+0.056$]. The fused-versus-additive comparison changes the trained parameterization and its optimization as well as the interaction capacity, so interaction-specific decision value remains unidentified. In parameter space the fused FiLM is close to additive: the interaction term is 0.26--0.43\% of per-block $\gamma$ variance. For $J$ the gap is $+0.013$ [$+0.004$, $+0.022$] (I). The additive code costs 1.14--2.02$\times$ the fused code's rollout error at $t=225$ across the nine requests. No code beats the no-model prior: fused request mean $-$ prior $=+0.001$ [$-0.025$, $+0.026$]. One factored seed (20260908) ended in a typed training failure, so the factored contrasts use two seeds.
\end{revblock}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\rev{Predicate feedback and the macro audit}}{Predicate feedback and the macro audit}}
\label{sec:app:res:macro}

\begin{revblock}
\nopagebreak
\textbf{Macro observability (\#113).} The primary model's own macro head reads macro F1 0.000 on the evaluation stream. A redesigned temporal macro audit head (four-carrier history, one head per $\Delta$, trained with half of its landing carriers taken from the primary model's one-step predictions) reaches macro F1 0.157 [0.151, 0.164] on parsed carriers, 0.113 [0.107, 0.119] on one-step predictions and 0.015 [0.014, 0.015] on open-loop rollouts (11,680 evaluation shots, 400 levels). These are audit-head measurements, not the primary model's macro head. Description labels identify supervised predicate-feedback modes, not semantic understanding.

\textbf{Macro against continuous under four success verdicts (\#120).} Averaged over $\Delta\in\{1,5,15\}$ and paired per level and seed, macro-minus-continuous grid AUC is $-0.012$ [$-0.019$, $-0.004$] when success is pig removal, $-0.024$ [$-0.029$, $-0.018$] when it is a destroyed block, $-0.036$ [$-0.048$, $-0.025$] when it is total displacement of at least 0.5 world units, and $-0.078$ [$-0.091$, $-0.065$] when it is a bird--block or bird--pig contact by frame 150. All four are N.

\textbf{Predicate feedback interventions (\#115).} Replacing the predicted predicates fed back into the trunk with engine labels (oracle feedback) changes $J$'s grid AUC by $-0.081$ [$-0.091$, $-0.071$] for micro and $-0.014$ [$-0.020$, $-0.008$] for macro. Removing the feedback changes it by $+0.002$ [$0.000$, $+0.005$] (micro) and $-0.004$ [$-0.008$, $+0.000$] (macro). A label-free micro variant, trained without the micro predicate loss, is non-inferior on the grid only ($J-T$ gap difference $-0.003$ [$-0.015$, $+0.008$]); on offset and angle its fixed-request mean ranks far worse. Removing the fed-back predicates leaves $J$ nearly unchanged, while correct engine labels lower it. This pattern is consistent with a model tuned to its own predicted predicates that draws little decision-relevant information from them.
\end{revblock}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\rev{Novelty transfer}}{Novelty transfer}}
\label{sec:app:res:novelty}

\begin{revblock}
\nopagebreak
\textbf{Retuning under novel physics (\#107).} The novelty protocol pools seven NovPhy novelty types (appearance change, fan, stronger air turbulence, magnet, mirrored slingshot, inverse gravity, inverse air turbulence) into 122 mixed-outcome evaluation levels $\times$ 3 seeds. Intervals cluster by evaluation level. Policy-only retuning on novel fit levels changes $J_{\mathrm{dec}}$'s AUC by $-0.007$ [$-0.021$, $+0.006$] relative to zero-shot (N), whereas world-model-stack retuning improves it by $+0.151$ [$+0.097$, $+0.206$] (S). For $J$, policy-only retuning gives $-0.057$ [$-0.079$, $-0.034$] (N). Under these novelties, retuning the request policy does not improve AUC; retuning the predictor does.

\textbf{Paired novel levels (\#128).} On 72 paired novel/normal level pairs (novelty types 1--7 pooled), the direct success predictors score 0.5776 [0.5269, 0.6272] (A1), 0.5172 [0.4661, 0.5681] (A2), 0.5174 (A2-native) and 0.5690 (A4); $U$ scores 0.4743 [0.4354, 0.5119], $C^{*}$ 0.4551 and the no-model prior 0.6022 [0.5459, 0.6559]. Every ranker loses AUC from the paired normal side to the novel side. These numbers are descriptive and enter no disposition.
\end{revblock}
