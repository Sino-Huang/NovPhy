\section{Setting and Formulation}
\label{sec:mth:setting}

An \emph{action-sparse persistent-effect environment} proceeds in discrete fixed steps. Interventions are sparse: an agent acts at a few steps, and the physical state then evolves autonomously for many steps, through collision onset and active rearrangement before settling. A prediction decision arises at each eligible nonterminal state before a candidate intervention. Because one intervention's consequences unfold long after it is issued, decisions differ in how far ahead they need to look and in how much detail the answer is useful. The environment class makes no assumption that these demands are homogeneous across a rollout. Whether they are homogeneous is what the experiments measure.

We instantiate this class on NovPhy, a published benchmark that evaluates how agents detect and adapt to novel physical dynamics across scenarios \citep{pinto2024novphy}. Benchmark and program are separate. NovPhy supplies the scenarios, the generator families, and the novelty levels. Its published findings belong to the benchmark. BG-NS-JEPA, the in-progress program studied here, supplies the models, the policies, and every number we report. Its scenario families serve as comparison strata throughout: the original single-force and multiple-forces families, the rolling and sliding families added for breadth, and matched appearance-novelty pairs. One out-of-family replication transfers the same contract to CLEVRER \citep{yi2020clevrer} under a remapped readout vocabulary (Section~\ref{sec:exp:clevrer}).

Each decision state receives a prediction request with two parameters. The \emph{requested horizon} $\Delta \in \{1, 5, 15\}$ is the number of fixed steps the decision asks the model to advance. Later sections and the claim registry write the requested horizon as $h$, and $h \equiv \Delta$ throughout. The \emph{output-description mode} $\alpha \in \{\mathrm{continuous}, \mathrm{micro}, \mathrm{macro}\}$ selects which supervised prediction target is queried and scored at that decision: \emph{continuous} exposes the predictive latent used for recursive continuation, \emph{micro} queries fine transition relations such as contact and support, and \emph{macro} queries coarse state or event predicates, in the released vocabulary \texttt{steady-state} and \texttt{structure-unstable}. The nine $(\Delta, \alpha)$ pairs form the evaluation surface. All nine run over one continuous latent, which carries every rollout between decisions. Modes are supervised readouts over that carrier, never alternative state representations, and no mode changes spatial resolution. Requested and effective horizon are distinct quantities. If the scenario terminates $k < \Delta$ steps after the decision, scoring clamps the offset to $k$. The request stays $\Delta$, and we never report the clamped value as the requested one.

\subsection{The Estimand Taxonomy}
\label{sec:mth:estimands}

Comparisons across the nine-pair surface differ in what they hold fixed and what they let vary. A single aggregate would conflate three distinct questions: whether symbol-supervised training changes the shared carrier, whether executing a symbolic readout changes prediction at a fixed checkpoint, and whether per-decision selection improves on fixed pairs. The three require different evidence, and an answer to one does not transfer to the others. We define each below as a contrast in which exactly one factor differs.

Each estimand varies exactly one factor. The \emph{training effect} $\tau_{\mathrm{tr}}(\Delta)$ varies the training recipe. It compares a model trained with symbol-supervised readouts (the hybrid arm) against an independently trained pure-continuous model, both executing the fixed continuous pair $(\Delta, \mathrm{continuous})$. The executed readout and the selection policy are identical. The \emph{symbolic-execution effect} $\tau_{\mathrm{ex}}(\Delta, \alpha)$ varies the executed readout. It compares a fixed symbolic readout $(\Delta, \alpha)$ against the fixed continuous readout at the same $\Delta$, inside one hybrid checkpoint. The trained model and selection policy are identical. The \emph{adaptation effect} $\tau_{\mathrm{ad}}$ varies the selection schedule. It compares a per-decision selection policy against fixed-pair controls within an arm at matched training. Checkpoints and readouts are identical per pair.

Classification is mechanical. For any contrast, examine what differs between its two sides: the training recipe alone gives a training effect, the readout at a shared checkpoint gives a symbolic-execution effect, and the selection schedule at shared checkpoints gives an adaptation effect. Under this rule, every quantitative claim in the paper attaches to exactly one estimand. Condition labels are part of estimand identity, so zero-shot and few-shot contrasts are separate cells and are never pooled. Each experiments subsection header names its estimand and its family. Two estimand switches occur in the paper, and both are flagged where they happen. The confirmatory result (Section~\ref{sec:exp:confirmatory}) is a paired gain in mean duration-weighted authoritative endpoint carrier MSE over eligible teacher-forced decision states, not a ranking regret. Sections~\ref{sec:exp:development} through \ref{sec:exp:external} quote normalized ranking regret against end-of-window replay cost. The out-of-family replication (Section~\ref{sec:exp:clevrer}) switches estimand again, to recursive position MSE at frozen annotation endpoints, and runs no candidate inventory on CLEVRER. The taxonomy extends beyond this program: any evaluation with a carrier, supervised readouts, and a selection policy can classify its contrasts the same way.

\section{The Pre-Registered Matched-System Protocol}
\label{sec:mth:protocol}

Section~\ref{sec:exp:anatomy} and the closed-loop bound discussed in Section~\ref{sec:disc} were produced under one pre-registered protocol. The protocol fixes, before any model outcome exists, which units are scored, which failures count, how arms are trained and accounted for, and which disposition token labels each outcome. We name its elements once here. The experiments sections invoke them by name, Appendix~\ref{sec:app:protocol} records the mechanics, and Appendix~\ref{sec:app:repro} the validation commands.

The protocol's design discipline is \textbf{frozen plans} (every campaign, training run, and evaluation run executes a plan frozen before launch and re-hashed at launch, refusing the run after any source edit); \textbf{sealed membership} (cell membership, held-out state identities, and the candidate inventory are pinned before any rendered run or model score, and no outcome moves membership); \textbf{typed terminal failures} (every scheduled unit ends admissible or as a typed failure with a recorded kind, retained and reported per cell, never silently dropped or worst-cased); and \textbf{equal-update training with work reporting} (paired arms share minibatches and update budgets, the continuous width is matched to the hybrid parameter count within a width tolerance, \texttt{training\_compute\_matched=false} is disclosed, and every cross-system contrast is work-reported). Its scoring and reporting discipline is \textbf{paired descriptive inference} (all contrasts report mean paired differences over paired units with descriptive 95\% paired-bootstrap intervals that support no superiority decision, and record a selection-optimism flag where a reference policy is selected on its scoring data); a \textbf{declared candidate inventory} (13 candidate actions per state, one reference drag plus 12 release-angle actions, are pinned before scoring, typed branch failures remove candidates without refill, and CLEVRER runs no inventory); \textbf{right-censored endpoints} (task costs are end-of-window costs at declared endpoints on mostly right-censored branches under a recorded stable-terminal absorption convention, never presented as settled costs); \textbf{every attempted cell reported} (the accounting is fixed in advance and reported in full, 208/208 normal-mechanics branches attempted, 186/208 appearance-novelty branches admissible on the novel side, 34/104 admissible on the parallel normal-side campaign with all 70 contention-era typed failures retained, and 480 CLEVRER windows with zero typed failures); and a \textbf{disposition vocabulary} (each executed question ends in exactly one verbatim token, \texttt{supported}, \texttt{not\_supported\_by\_this\_experiment}, or \texttt{readiness\_or\_precision\_insufficient}, and a negative is never restated as ``no significant difference'').

These rules buy one property: the boundary curves of Section~\ref{sec:exp:anatomy} cannot arise from post-hoc cell selection. The development anatomy reports the original-family rows that hurt the hybrid beside the new-family rows that help it (Section~\ref{sec:exp:development}), and the claim registry lists each claim with its disposition (Section~\ref{sec:syn:picture}). Closed-loop behavior is scored under the same contract by matched gameplay systems, extended additively with fixed-horizon, hybrid fixed-mode, and no-model ordinal-prior arms. These systems are infrastructure, and we claim no benefit from them.
