\section{Protocol Mechanics and Measurement Conventions}
\label{sec:app:protocol}

This section defines the protocol lifecycle and the measurement conventions that every downstream section uses.
Every executed artifact in this paper is produced under a frozen plan. The lifecycle has five stages: freeze, validate, execute, publish, exact-validate. At the freeze stage the runner writes \texttt{plan.json}, which pins membership, templates, seeds, estimands, metrics, contrasts, compute accounting, and stop rules; a freeze contains actual numerical values, and approximations or example values in place of values are banned. After the freeze, loading the plan re-hashes the source tree and the runner refuses to execute after any source edit. The validate stage checks the plan before any outcome is computed, execution then runs every scheduled cell, and publication writes the \#77 layout (\texttt{summary.json}, \texttt{comparisons.csv}, \texttt{findings.md}) beside the producing module. The exact-validate stage reruns a \texttt{-{}-validate} mode that recomputes every published table from the artifacts and must exit 0. \rev{Appendix~\ref{sec:app:repro} reports each artifact's last observed validation status and date.} Campaign runners additionally write \texttt{inventory.json}, \texttt{campaign.json}, \texttt{ledger.json}, \texttt{coverage.json}, per-branch \texttt{markers/}, \texttt{receipts/}, and \texttt{attempts/}, and their \texttt{-{}-validate} mode re-derives \texttt{coverage.json} from the retained ledger. A recorded reseal guard regenerates a plan through its own prepare module and aborts unless the only drift is recorded source text, with membership, branches, and prior-exposure byte-identical. Superseded pre-review plans stay on disk and were never used for any outcome.

A typed terminal failure is a scheduled cell retained with its failure kind when it cannot complete. Every scheduled cell is executed or retained as a typed terminal failure. There is no outcome-conditioned exclusion, retry, replacement, or re-freeze. A terminal-incomplete ledger refuses top-up permanently, and all failure records are kept. Allowances were recorded per ticket, and an unfunded cell is recorded \texttt{unsupported}, never dropped.

Measurement discipline is uniform. All paired contrasts carry bootstrap intervals over paired units, labelled descriptive, and no interval is read as a significance test. No multiplicity adjustment is applied. Zero-shot and few-shot conditions are reported separately and never pooled. \rev{Inventories are scored separately and never pooled.}

Paired checkpoints use three seeds, 20260908, 20260909, and 20260910, across the retraining families. Campaign generation and engine seeds are pinned disjoint from all earlier ranges in each frozen plan. \rev{The sealed NovPhy split draws fresh level seeds from bases 765,000,000 and 765,500,000 (Section~\ref{sec:app:proto:split}).} Table~\ref{tab:app:checkpoints} inventories the per-seed artifacts.

The protocol's design discipline is \textbf{frozen plans} (every campaign, training run, and evaluation run executes a plan frozen before launch and re-hashed at launch, refusing the run after any source edit); \textbf{sealed membership} (cell membership, held-out state identities, and the candidate inventory are pinned before any rendered run or model score, and no outcome moves membership); \textbf{typed terminal failures} (every scheduled unit ends admissible or as a typed failure with a recorded kind, retained and reported per cell, never silently dropped or worst-cased); and \textbf{equal-update training with work reporting} (paired arms share minibatches and update budgets, and the continuous width is \rev{the width whose parameter count is nearest the hybrid's, so the two predictors have similar, not equal, sizes}). Its scoring discipline is \textbf{paired descriptive inference} (contrasts report mean paired differences over paired units with descriptive 95\% paired-bootstrap intervals); a \textbf{declared candidate inventory} (\rev{each level's inventories are pinned before scoring, typed branch failures remove candidates without refill,} and CLEVRER runs no inventory); and \textbf{every attempted cell reported} (the accounting is fixed in advance and reported in full, with all typed failures retained).

\subsection{Sealed NovPhy split and exposure checks}
\label{sec:app:proto:split}

The cohort holds 1,000 normal-physics levels from two familiar NovPhy generator families (\texttt{type010103} and \texttt{type010105}). In fixed roster order each family is divided, level-disjointly, into 240 predictor-fit, 60 policy-fit and 200 evaluation levels, giving 480/120/400. The plan was frozen before any capture, and its validator re-derives every level and re-checks disjointness. Level seeds were drawn fresh from the bases 765,000,000 and 765,500,000 and checked against 27 reserved ranges, with zero collisions in any of five exposure classes over 83 earlier exposure sources. Before any outcome existed, 160 candidate seeds were rejected because a level object fell outside the encoder's frozen 18-slot vocabulary. The captures share a stride of 50 native engine steps per carrier frame. Predictor-fit captures comprise 13,920 launches (13,919 complete; one typed failure). Policy-fit captures comprise 3,480 launches, all complete. Evaluation captures comprise 11,680 launches, all complete, with zero typed failures and zero incomplete launches.

The split is identity-disjoint, not distribution-shifted. Both families appear in every role, and a nearest-fit pixel lookup reaches 0.9039 [0.8838, 0.9231] grid AUC (Appendix~\ref{sec:app:external}).

\subsection{Inventories and mixed-outcome counts}

Grid is a $4\times4$ Cartesian grid of drag vectors (horizontal drag $\in\{-40,-80,-120,-160\}$, vertical $\in\{5,30,55,80\}$, release after 1000\,ms), 16 candidates on every level. Offset is an offset-angle sweep at radius 80\,px (11 candidates). Angle is a launch-angle sweep at radius 80\,px (13 candidates). Power is an angle-by-launch-power sweep (20 candidates). The four inventories form a union of 60 distinct launches. Every evaluation level carries the grid, and the first 60 evaluation levels of each family (120 of 400) also carry offset, angle and power. Predictor-fit and policy-fit levels carry angle and grid (29 launches). The depth of the non-grid inventories was set before capture from development variance at a design margin of 0.05 AUC.

Mixed-outcome evaluation levels number 311 (grid), 76 (offset), 78 (angle) and 47 (power), against frozen requirements of 293, 32, 55 and 53. Power is the only inventory below its requirement. With three predictor seeds the grid has 933 scored cells. Grid is the primary inventory of every study, and offset, angle and power are descriptive.

\subsection{Scoring path}
\label{sec:app:proto:scoring}

\textbf{Anchors.} For each evaluation level, the decision-frame carrier $z_0$ is the encoder's parse of frame 0 of the level's first complete candidate launch, made after the freeze and stored once. Every arm on every seed reads this anchor store. The carrier has no prior frame, so its motion entries are unavailable.

\textbf{Rollout and cost.} Each candidate is rolled out from $z_0$ to $t=225$ under the arm's fixed request or policy. The endpoint is scored by the tie-free cost of Equation~\ref{eq:taskcost}: unclipped pig presence minus $0.1\times$ the Euclidean displacement of the pig-slot centre between $z_0$ and the endpoint, in normalized screen units. A nonfinite endpoint is a typed failure and is excluded from its cell (the predictor scoring of $U$ recorded a nonfinite share of 0.0). Cell AUC follows Equation~\ref{eq:auc} with ties counted one half.

\textbf{Deterministic scoring.} Every scoring runs in a process that did not train, under a fixed policy (cuDNN benchmark off, cuDNN deterministic on, TF32 off for convolutions and matrix products), and each record stores this policy. Replication checks compare pig presence, pig displacement and tie-free cost within a parse tolerance of $10^{-3}$.

\textbf{Count cost (submitted records only).} The submitted checkpoints ranked launches by
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{cost}_{\mathrm{count}}(a)=1000\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{pig}}}\bar p_i(\hat z_K)+\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{block}}}\bar p_i(\hat z_K),
\label{eq:select}
\end{equation}
where $\bar p_i(z)$ is slot $i$'s presence entry clipped to $[0,1]$, and $\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{pig}}$ and $\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{block}}$ index the target (pig) and block slots. Because presences saturate, many candidates receive equal costs. The original submitted records used it. The evaluation-split rerun of the submitted checkpoints uses the tie-free cost as its primary cost and reports the count cost as a secondary reading (Appendix~\ref{sec:app:development}). No primary-stack result uses it.

\subsection{Statistics, decision rule and guards}

\textbf{Intervals.} Every interval is a descriptive 95\% percentile bootstrap with 10,000 draws (PCG64 seed 7201). The resampling unit is the level (the member in submitted records), and all seeds of a level are resampled together, so three predictor seeds are not three independent samples of levels. Intervals carry no multiplicity adjustment and are not significance tests.

\textbf{Decision rule.} A study freezes its contrasts and its guards. With $\delta=0.02$, a contrast is \texttt{supported} (main text: supported) when all guards pass, its mean is at least $\delta$ and its 95\% lower bound exceeds zero; \texttt{not\_supported\_by\_this\_experiment} (not supported) when all guards pass and its upper bound is below $\delta$; and \texttt{readiness\_or\_precision\_insufficient} (insufficient) otherwise. Any failed guard turns every token of that study into the insufficient reading. Non-grid inventories are descriptive and carry no token.

\textbf{Guards of the primary-stack studies.} Each study freezes its own list.
\begin{itemize}
\item Predictor scoring (\#108): G1 isolation (every scored launch is an evaluation-split capture; every wrong anchor is a predictor-fit level); G2 scoring policy on every record and on the anchor store; G3 chronology (freeze $\le$ anchor parse $\le$ first score); G4 checkpoint binding (every scored checkpoint's hash equals the training handoff). Here a failed guard refuses publication.
\item Request policies (\#101): G1 isolation (policy-fit pool disjoint from predictor-fit and evaluation); G2 scoring policy; G3 chronology (freeze $\le$ training $\le$ selection $\le$ first score); G4 checkpoint binding; G5 replication of the \#108 rows for $U$ and $C^*$.
\item Same-checkpoint controls (\#114): G1--G4 as in \#101; G5 replication of the fixed and reused-$J$ rows of \#101 and of $U$ and $C^*$; G6 every restricted and $J_{\mathrm{cont}}^{\mathrm{NovPhy}}$ schedule uses only its allowed requests.
\item Live evaluation (\#102): G1 scoring policy; G2 decision determinism (re-rankings identical across shots and equal to the retained offline rows); G3 chronology; G4 every live decision frame parses within $10^{-3}$ of the stored anchor; G5 execution integrity. G4 failed (Section~\ref{sec:app:proto:live}).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Guards of the submitted-checkpoint rerun (\#104).} G1 smoke replication of the submitted records; G2 anchor-store hash and parse policy; G3 verdict resolution 1.0 and nonfinite share at most 0.1; G4 at most 0.2 of mixed grid cells fully tied for $J$ or $F^*$; G5 $J$ non-modal step share at least 0.1 and $J$'s ranking not identical to its modal fixed request on at least 0.25 of cells; G6 primary half-width at most 0.02; G7 scoring policy on every record; and compute caps. Appendix~\ref{sec:app:development} reports its readings.

\textbf{Scored once.} Each frozen arm is scored once on the evaluation split after its freeze. In the same-checkpoint study (\#114), a duplicate launch re-executed some fixed-request prefixes, which broke literal scored-once execution. The retained deterministic records were verified, no rule changed afterwards, and $J-J_{\mathrm{cont}}^{\mathrm{NovPhy}}$ is therefore not a pristine independent replication.

\textbf{Label and outcome usage.} Engine labels and outcomes enter the primary stack in five places. (i) The encoder was trained on engine-projected slot, relation and scene labels of frames from 12 earlier 50-step levels; it reads no launch outcome. (ii) The predictor's window loss uses engine predicate labels of predictor-fit frames, and its ranking loss uses predictor-fit outcomes. (iii) The no-model prior uses predictor-fit outcomes. (iv) The training and $\lambda$ selection of $J_{\mathrm{dec}}$ (decision-valued labels) and $J_{\mathrm{pg}}$ (REINFORCE on policy-fit AUC), and the choice of the remaining-frame input variant, use policy-fit outcomes. (v) Development outcomes on the earlier 50-step captures chose the training recipe, the primary fixed request $U$ and the continuous baseline $C^*$ before the sealed NovPhy split was scored. Evaluation outcomes enter only scoring and explicitly outcome-selected references ($F_{\mathrm{hind}}$, $F^*$, observed-frontier summaries, the inventory oracle).

\subsection{Live retry-from-start protocol}
\label{sec:app:proto:live}

All 400 evaluation levels hold one bird, so a level ends after one shot. An episode allows at most three shots. Each shot resets the level to its start state. Before each shot, the arm ranks the 16 grid launches from the decision-frame anchor, removes launches that already failed in this episode, and executes its top remaining launch live in the engine. The episode ends at the first pass (live engine pig removal) or after three shots. Because the state never changes, re-planning amounts to excluding failed launches, and no arm controls a changing state.

Arms are the nine fixed requests on $F_\theta$, $C^*$, the request policies, the no-model prior, uniform random (one fixed permutation per seed and level) and the inventory oracle, which executes recorded passing launches lowest ordinal first and is nondeployable. Model and random arms play three seeds, giving 1,200 level--seed episodes each. The prior and the oracle are seed-free. Their 400 distinct episodes are repeated per seed in the 1,200-cell table and are not additional executions. One live execution serves every episode that chooses the same level and launch. Together with a separately reported 40-level novelty set, 5,123 live executions served all episodes. Of these, 5,106 completed, and the 17 typed engine failures count as not passed. Every completed live verdict equals the recorded capture verdict of the same level and launch (5,106 of 5,106).

Guard G4 failed: 25 of the 5,106 completed live decision frames, on 5 levels (three evaluation levels and two levels of the novelty set), parsed up to 0.0032 from the stored anchor, against a tolerance of $10^{-3}$, because these levels' decision frames are not pixel-stable across engine runs. Every formal live contrast is therefore insufficient, and live results are descriptive. A post-hoc re-ranking from each distinct live decision frame left the first three picks unchanged in 351 of 351 comparisons.

\subsection{Pixel-benchmark protocol}
\label{sec:app:proto:pixel}

\textbf{Membership.} On CLEVRER the parser keys one slot to each colour--material pair (16 keyed slots and 2 empty), and videos that repeat a key or violate the slot contract are excluded: 37.22\% pooled over the two releases. The study fits on 6,285 training-release videos (IDs 0--9999) and evaluates on 3,132 validation-release videos (IDs 10000--14999), with zero ID overlap; per-video ID lists are frozen in the plan. It therefore describes a parser-compatible subpopulation, not full-release CLEVRER. On Physion-Dominoes the study uses the red--yellow model release: the \texttt{dynamics} release (2,000 trials) fits predictors and policies, the \texttt{readout} release (996) fits the object-contact-prediction readout, and the \texttt{testing} release (150) evaluates.

\textbf{Overlap audit.} No Physion trial shares its full static parameter set, colour included, across releases. With the appearance-only colour dropped, 20 of the 150 testing trials share every physical parameter with a fitting trial (15 with a dynamics trial, 17 with a readout trial, 12 with both), and 4 testing trials have a fitting partner within $10^{-3}$ world units at every frame. The official split therefore measures transfer to new colours, not to independent physical realizations. A physically group-disjoint Physion evaluation was not run.

\textbf{Carrier and time base.} The parser is the primary stack's encoder architecture, trained separately per benchmark from pixels with its recipe (12 epochs, batch 64, AdamW $10^{-3}$). It feeds the same 236-value layout with motion as centre difference over elapsed time. CLEVRER carriers take every video frame (0.0390625\,s, 128 frames); Physion carriers take every third native frame (0.1\,s). Frames past a trial's end repeat the last frame.

\textbf{Parser fidelity.} On held-out frames, against annotation targets: CLEVRER presence F1 0.9932, centre error 16.83\,px (of 480), kind accuracy 0.9844; Physion presence F1 0.9204, centre error 45.88\,px (of 256), kind accuracy 0.8850.

\textbf{Endpoints and metric.} Rollouts start from one parsed carrier and run to $t=120$ (CLEVRER) or $t=90$ (Physion). Position error is parser-relative: the mean squared error of predicted centres against the centres of the parser's carrier at the same frame, not simulator ground truth. Intervals resample seeds and clips together (10,000 draws, PCG64 seed 7201). Persistence and constant-velocity references were not evaluated on this protocol; no comparison with them is claimed.
