\paragraph{Adapting granularity.}
Horizon-adaptive world models jump to predictable or key frames \citep{jayaraman2019tap,pertsch2020keyin,kim2019vta}, condition on the time step \citep{nhu2025tawm}, or segment time by context change or surprise \citep{gumbsch2024thick,iiyama2026sunta}, while what they predict stays fixed; VisualPredicator instead invents predicates over a fixed schedule \citep{liang2025visualpredicator}.

Hierarchical models fix which time scale goes with which level of abstraction: H-JEPA assigns short-term, low-level prediction to one level and longer-term, higher-level to the next \citep[\S4.4, \S4.6]{lecun2022path}, and Clockwork VAE, MSPred and Director fix these scales in the architecture \citep{saxena2021clockwork,villarcorrales2022mspred,hafner2022director}. SALT learns both abstractions jointly, but once, as a fixed repertoire for skill planning \citep{huang2026salt}. Others select per state, but a different variable: \citet{wang2023dynamic} choose a model resolution at each MPC step over a fixed horizon, and MuSix routes each state to one world model in a mixture \citep{jang2026musix}. Granularity thus changes by switching level, skill or model, whereas simulators adjust step and detail within one run \citep{mirtich2000timewarp,carlson1997simulation}. To our knowledge, no world model chooses horizon and description jointly, per prediction, over one shared latent state.

\paragraph{Action-sparse physics.}
Physical-reasoning benchmarks pose one-shot interventions or passive dynamics \citep{bakhtin2019phyre,bear2021physion,yi2020clevrer}, and forward-prediction agents on PHYRE use object- or pixel-based models of fixed granularity \citep{girdhar2020forward}. To our knowledge, NovPhy \citep{pinto2024novphy} has not been used to evaluate a learned world model; Appendix~\ref{sec:app:related} gives more detail.
