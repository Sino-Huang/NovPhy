### W1 (a): CLEVRER train/eval scene overlap

**You are right.** The submitted rollout table scored the 12 CLEVRER training scenes in-sample (we confirmed this in the post-submission audit: the in-sample fold is exactly the submitted setting; [#117](https://github.com/Sino-Huang/NovPhy/issues/117) item 5 labels it as such). - **Held-out pixel benchmarks rebuilt from scratch** ([#106](https://github.com/Sino-Huang/NovPhy/issues/106)): CLEVRER train 6,285 / validation 3,132 videos (the 37.22% of videos whose colour×material slot keys repeat are excluded by a pre-declared rule; test answers are withheld by the evaluation server, so validation is the scored split); Physion-Dominoes dynamics/readout/testing archives with a sha256 manifest per file.
- **Leakage audit**: 0 pairs of splits share a full static parameter set (layout, colours, masses, frictions, push) on either benchmark. Two qualifications we report rather than hide: (i) 6 fixed CLEVRER layouts recur across splits as an appearance-level overlap; (ii) on Physion, once colour is excluded, **20/150 testing trials share every physical parameter with a fitting-split trial and 4 are near-duplicates (≤1e-3 world units at every frame)** — so official-split Physion results do not establish generalization to independent physical realizations. This qualification is printed next to the table in the revision.
- **Result on held-out scenes**: the submitted direction replicates — rollout error is lowest at Δ = 15 on both benchmarks, for the pixel parser and all description levels; parser fidelity on held-out frames is presence F1 0.993 / centre error 16.8 px (CLEVRER) and 0.920 / 45.9 px (Physion).

Scene/split IDs and manifests are committed (`data/clevrer/manifest.json`, `data/physion-dominoes/manifest.json`), and the revision lists them in the appendix.

### W1 (b): too few NovPhy evaluation levels

**Also right.** The submission scored 7–14 mixed levels per launch set. The sealed split ([#104](https://github.com/Sino-Huang/NovPhy/issues/104)) replaces this with 400 fresh evaluation levels and 11,680 engine-verdicted launches: **grid 311, offset 76, angle 78, power 47** mixed levels per inventory × 3 seeds. Contrasts that fail their precision guard are labelled as such rather than interpreted.

### W2 (a): action-ranking results near random; description levels inconsistent

Both facts are confirmed by our own audit, and we can now explain and bound them.

- **Near-chance AUCs were substantially a measurement artifact.** Two independent defects: the frozen parser never saw the pig (0/859 decision frames), and the count cost tied in most cells, so the submitted AUCs (0.40–0.63) ranked noise. After both fixes, on the **held-out** split the *submitted checkpoint* still ranks near chance (J grid AUC **0.555 [0.539, 0.571]** vs F\* 0.542 and the hindsight-best fixed request F-15-continuous 0.639) — so your reading of the submitted model stands. With perception fixed and the predictor retrained (#112 recipe), launch ranking clears chance decisively: **0.744 [0.727, 0.762]** vs a no-model prior of 0.720 and a matched continuous baseline C\* of 0.783 ([#108](https://github.com/Sino-Huang/NovPhy/issues/108) Gate B; pass rule grid AUC ≥ 0.65, lower bound > 0.55, pre-declared).
- **The policy's own gains over fixed requests are weak — we now say they are unresolved.** On held-out levels, J − F\* = +0.013 [−0.016, +0.043]; the best decision-valued variant still trails the best fixed request (−0.016 [−0.020, −0.012]). The revision states: no selection policy, label type, or state-dependence variant beat the best fixed request by δ on any inventory.
- **Description levels are inconsistent because the macro level is currently unreadable in rollouts.** The macro predicates are now observable at the anchor (held-out macro F1 0.157 parsed), but through open-loop rollouts macro F1 collapses to **0.015**, and the macro description level has no ranking advantage under the control verdict or any of three alternative verdicts (macro − continuous between −0.012 and −0.078; [#113](https://github.com/Sino-Huang/NovPhy/issues/113), [#120](https://github.com/Sino-Huang/NovPhy/issues/120)). Micro is the opposite story at the perception level: a label-free micro bottleneck is non-inferior to engine-labelled micro ([#115](https://github.com/Sino-Huang/NovPhy/issues/115)). The revision narrows the description-axis claim accordingly (see Q2).

### Q1. Why were CLEVRER training and evaluation samples drawn from the same scenes? How is generalization assessed?

The in-sample choice was an error of scope, not intent: the pixel benchmarks were built as rollout-accuracy diagnostics on annotation carriers, and we reused the 12 scenes rather than building held-out splits. On held-out CLEVRER scenes the submitted Table-1 direction (Δ = 15 lowest rollout error) reproduces.

### Q2. What benefit do the symbolic description levels offer over continuous prediction alone?

Measured on the sealed split, at matched checkpoints and matched (at-most-budget) compute ([#114](https://github.com/Sino-Huang/NovPhy/issues/114)):

- **Joint vs horizon-only twin** (same trunk, parameters, policy recipe, pool, seeds; six of nine logits masked to continuous): **J − J-cont = +0.0201 [0.0154, 0.0248]**, supported. Qualifications we state with the number: J-cont cannot reach the declared budgets in the frozen λ-sweep (endpoint-clamped, at-most-budget comparison, not equal spend), and a scoring incident (duplicate deterministic workers, disclosed in `execution-incident.json`) broke literal scored-once execution for some fixed-arm prefixes; deterministic endpoints were re-verified bit-exactly.
- **Against replaying J's own schedule with continuous substituted at every step**: +0.0037 [−0.0021, +0.0095] — no δ-level α-selection gain. Against the matched continuous *predictor's* adaptive policy: +0.0111 [0.0050, 0.0174] — not supported.
- **Macro offers no ranking benefit today** (−0.039 unit−C\*; no macro advantage under any verdict, [#120](https://github.com/Sino-Huang/NovPhy/issues/120)), and macro predicates barely survive rollouts (F1 0.015). Micro feedback is robust to label removal (non-inferior) but engine-truth *oracle* feedback hurts (−0.345 on fixed micro requests) — the adapter learned the head's output distribution, not predicate semantics ([#115](https://github.com/Sino-Huang/NovPhy/issues/115)).

So the honest answer: a small, qualified joint-over-horizon-only margin at matched compute, micro descriptions as a robust (label-free-able) readout axis, and **no demonstrated macro benefit** — the revision claims exactly this and no more.

### Q3. Do the weak action-ranking results translate into meaningful improvements in task success?

We ran the closed-loop study ([#102](https://github.com/Sino-Huang/NovPhy/issues/102)): every arm plans from the level's decision frame and its chosen launch executes **live in the engine** (5,123 live executions; 19,360 episodes; live verdicts matched recorded verdicts 5,106/5,106). Pass@3 over 400 held-out levels, 3 shots: F-15-continuous **0.478 [0.434, 0.523]**, C\* 0.466, J_dec 0.445 [0.403, 0.487], the submitted unit 0.424, J 0.394 [0.352, 0.438], horizon-only C-J 0.346, no-model prior 0.323, random 0.198, oracle 0.775. Declared contrasts: J − C-J +0.048 [0.028, 0.069], J_dec − J +0.051 [0.028, 0.075], unit − C\* **−0.042 [−0.060, −0.025]**. Two honest caveats: (i) a frozen frame-identity guard failed (25/5,106 live decision frames parse ≤0.0032 from their anchors; a post-hoc re-ranking check left the first three picks unchanged 351/351), so **every closed-loop contrast carries the stop token** — we report the descriptive numbers, not a claim; (ii) the answer to your question is therefore: the ranking gains do translate into a task-success ordering in which fixed coarse requests ≥ adaptive policies, i.e., **we do not claim task-success improvements for the adaptive policy**. On native agents ([#132](https://github.com/Sino-Huang/NovPhy/issues/132)): classic AIBIRDS agents playing the same levels reach pass@3 0.217–0.307 — below the model-planning rows — but the protocols differ (agents re-choose freely; our planner excludes failed grid launches), so these rows are descriptive context, not a contrast.

### Q4. Can the approach be evaluated in more complex or closed-loop environments?

The single-bird retry-from-start closed loop above is what was feasible pre-deadline; it is a bounded, honest step (live engine execution, no replayed outcomes). Multi-decision closed-loop environments were not evaluated, and we flag this as a limitation in the revision rather than extrapolating. What we can say: the architecture's action-conditioning is exercised only once per episode on this task, and the H-JEPA result ([#123](https://github.com/Sino-Huang/NovPhy/issues/123)) shows action sparsity is precisely the regime where hierarchical latent predictors fail, which is evidence the setting is a meaningful stress test even though it is not a full control benchmark.

### W3 (and Q1). The time base: Δ in engine steps vs 50-step frames; Table 1 on held-out scenes

- **Clarification, and a scoped concession.** Your reading of §4 is correct, and the mismatch sits inside the original NovPhy corpus itself — it is not a cross-dataset artifact. Auditing the base corpus ([#71](https://github.com/Sino-Huang/NovPhy/issues/71)): of the 3,000 original training-role lineages, 2,400 were used for predictor fitting (600 reserved for controller supervision), each shot contributing up to four 60-**fixed-step** windows, with DP labels defined on endpoint carrier MSE; the early dynamics models were trained at 1/5/15-**native-step** horizons, while the deployment/N1 carriers use 50-native-step frames, so evaluation requests Δ ∈ {1, 5, 15} mean 50/250/750 native steps ([#76](https://github.com/Sino-Huang/NovPhy/issues/76)). For models trained on that corpus, training jumps of up to ~15 engine steps really were up to ~50× shorter than the longest evaluation jumps. We did not demonstrate any mechanism that bridges the two bases, and we will not claim one: the revision states the conversion explicitly (§4/§5 plus a table), scopes the submitted checkpoint's claims to the base it was trained on, and records that the post-submission Gate-B stack is trained *and* evaluated on a single declared base (E99 carriers at the 50-step stride, rollouts supervised to frame 225).
- **Elapsed time and the request policy — three signals the submitted text conflated.** (i) The carrier contains an elapsed-seconds channel (its motion features are centre differences divided by that elapsed time); this is where a 1-step and a 50-step transition differ at the predictor input. (ii) The request code's Δ selects the horizon. (iii) The policy receives a remaining-frames feature κ that saturates at 60 frames. We ablated (iii) and added an explicit elapsed-time feature at the *policy input* ([#119](https://github.com/Sino-Huang/NovPhy/issues/119)): unsaturated κ changes grid AUC by +0.0042 [−0.0005, +0.0088]; the added elapsed input by −0.001 [−0.006, +0.005]; the decision-valued policy is unchanged (−0.004); and training on 225-frame label windows *hurts* (−0.026 [−0.032, −0.019]). These ablations show the policy is insensitive to its own time inputs. They do **not** establish whether the predictor uses the carrier's elapsed channel to bridge the two time bases — that channel was not ablated, and we report this as an open limitation rather than a resolution. What is fixed: the sealed pipeline declares the 50-step stride in every manifest, and the request grid is anchored to it (sub-frame Δ deliberately not tested, [#116](https://github.com/Sino-Huang/NovPhy/issues/116)).
- **Table 1 recomputed on held-out scenes: done** ([#106](https://github.com/Sino-Huang/NovPhy/issues/106), plus the sealed NovPhy split): CLEVRER validation-only contexts, documented Physion-Dominoes testing trials, all nine requests including (15, macro); the Δ = 15 advantage reproduces on both. The policy's own schedule errors are reported too (J stays close to F-1 on rollout error). A persistence/constant-velocity reference row is **not yet in the table**; we agree it belongs there and will add it in the revision as a pure scoring change on the stored rollouts.

Thank you for the two strengths you note and for questions that map one-to-one onto experiments we have now run. Your Weakness 2 in particular is, in substance, confirmed by our own measurements; we answer each part.

### W1. Temporal-rate encoding (1 vs 50 engine steps per carrier frame) poorly characterized

Agreed, and now characterized. The carrier carries an elapsed-seconds channel (motion is defined as centre difference over elapsed time) — separate both from the request code's Δ and from the policy's remaining-frames feature, which saturates at 60 frames — and the submitted text neither separated these three signals nor stated the stride (1 carrier frame = 50 native steps). We ran the policy-input study ([#119](https://github.com/Sino-Huang/NovPhy/issues/119)): unsaturated remaining-frames +0.0042 [−0.0005, +0.0088]; an explicit elapsed-time input −0.001 [−0.006, +0.005]; longer (225-frame) label windows −0.026 [−0.032, −0.019]. Transferability of the rate encoding is therefore *not* demonstrated — and the policy-input ablations do not speak to whether the predictor exploits the carrier's elapsed channel, which we state as an open limitation rather than a finding — but the rate encoding is also not the binding limitation: ranking quality tracks predictor training updates, not the request set or the time inputs ([#116](https://github.com/Sino-Huang/NovPhy/issues/116): the same predictors gain +0.10 to +0.24 AUC from more updates per request, with the request set fixed). The revision documents the stride, reports both input variants, and scopes the claim to the tested base.

### W2. The compute comparison changes width/tokenization/utilization/dataset at once; the DP optimizes an additive fixed-context proxy

**Both parts are correct, and we now have the matched experiments.**

1. *Matched comparison* ([#114](https://github.com/Sino-Huang/NovPhy/issues/114)): J vs **J-cont** — identical trunk, parameters, initialization, tokenization, training data, pool, seeds and recipe; only the request mask differs. And J vs **C-J** — the matched continuous *predictor* with a matched controller (242→131→3 vs 242→128→9). Results: J − J-cont **+0.0201 [0.0154, 0.0248]** (supported, at-most-budget, incident-disclosed); J − C-J +0.0111 [0.0050, 0.0174] (not supported). The MAC decomposition (W2/Q4 answer to MSwm) shows the submitted saving was the trunk; the claim is withdrawn and replaced by the engine-truth frontier ([#103](https://github.com/Sino-Huang/NovPhy/issues/103)), on which the submitted operating point is dominated by C-F-15 (+0.078, supported) and by the zero-compute prior.
2. *The DP proxy*: yes — the submitted DP minimizes an additive, fixed-context surrogate — duration-weighted carrier MSE plus λ-normalized linear work; the exact recurrence is in our Q5 answer — not the true recursive, state-dependent rollout cost. We tested alternatives after submission ([#101](https://github.com/Sino-Huang/NovPhy/issues/101)): decision-valued labels (one-step lookahead over a base-policy continuation with per-state request deviations) improve the policy (J_dec − J = +0.040 [+0.030, +0.052], grid) but still trail the best fixed request (−0.016 [−0.020, −0.012]); policy-gradient training on imagined rollouts collapses to issuing a single fixed request at every step. The proxy is therefore not the binding limitation either — the predictor is — and the revision states this as an interpretation, backed by #116/#117 (update-limited ranking, no fit/evaluation leakage found).

### Q2. Which variable captures the 1-engine-step vs 50-engine-step distinction?

Two distinct inputs, and we now keep them separate: the carrier's elapsed-seconds channel (with motion normalized by it) — the only place a 1-step and a 50-step transition differ at the predictor input — and the policy's remaining-frames feature. The direct ablation is at the policy input ([#119](https://github.com/Sino-Huang/NovPhy/issues/119), summarized under W1): adding an explicit elapsed-time input there changes nothing beyond δ, and unsaturating the remaining-frames feature changes +0.004. The carrier's elapsed channel itself was **not** ablated, so we neither attribute rate sensitivity to Eq. ### Q5. What exactly does the DP optimize; how are round-2 successor contexts built for unvisited requests?

- **Objective.** The submitted DP is prediction-error DP over a teacher-forced window. At position t and request r with horizon Δ_r, the cost is **c(t, r) = Δ_r · mean((F(context_t, a, r) − z_{t+Δ_r})²) + λ · work(r)/work(reference)**, with λ = 1e-4 and the reference the (15, continuous) request; labels solve the backward recurrence **V(t) = min_r [c(t, r) + V(t + Δ_r)]** (infeasible past the window end; exact ties to the lower request index). It is precisely the additive fixed-context proxy you describe — duration-weighted carrier MSE plus normalized linear work — and its optimality guarantee holds **only** for that surrogate, not for the true recursive rollout cost. The revision now states this.
- **Successor contexts in the DAgger round.** Round-0 labels are computed on observed engine windows. In the DAgger round the window's context tensor is assembled by greedily rolling the round-0 policy: every position the policy *visits* has its context overwritten by the model's own predicted carrier, and unvisited positions retain the observed carriers; the DP is then recomputed on this fixed mixed context against the unchanged aligned true targets. So a request's successor at an unvisited position is observed, but the context the DP branches from after any visited step is already the model's prediction — a fixed-context surrogate either way, which is why we accept your characterization and claim no recursive-rollout optimality. The decision-valued variant ([#101](https://github.com/Sino-Huang/NovPhy/issues/101), introduced post-submission) instead rolls a base policy (F-15-macro in round 0; the round-0 policy in the DAgger round) and deviates one step to each feasible request, continuing to frame 225; its surrogate (a candidate-normalized softplus ranking loss) is disclosed in the revision, since 140/167 mixed policy cells contain a single success.
